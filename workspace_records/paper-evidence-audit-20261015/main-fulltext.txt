RA VEN-MCS: Target-Mass-Calibrated
Windowed-Asynchronous Federated Data
Completion for Mobile Crowdsensing under
Two-Stage Non-Random Selection
**** and Anfeng Liu
Abstract—Mobile crowdsensing completion learns from up-
dates selected by opportunity, observation, and usable-update
delivery. These mechanisms shape an arrival law that can differ
from the public deployment target. RA VEN-MCS reconstructs
a selection-corrected reference with a preconfigured opportunity
Design factor from pre-outcome histories and calibrates it within
the feasible aggregation set of each asynchronous window. We
distinguish deployment-target risk from arrival-weighted risk, es-
tablish ideal Design-on target-moment and group-mass recovery
under support and identification conditions, and prove unique
solvability and plug-in stability of the calibration program.
Paired component comparisons isolate the roles of correction and
feasibility: on SensorScope, projecting the corrected reference
improves group mismatch by+0.768percentage points over
uniform server weights, while the Design factor provides a further
+0.922points on the same active set. Station-based protocol
experiments and hybrid mobility replays characterize structural
alignment and its relationship to deployment-weighted prediction
error. The resulting framework connects the population repre-
sented by usable observations to the client mixtures that a finite
sensing window can realize.
Index Terms—Mobile crowdsensing, federated learning, data
completion, windowed asynchronous learning, non-random selec-
tion, selection bias, target-mass alignment.
I. INTRODUCTION
Mobile crowdsensing (MCS) collects urban and environ-
mental data from mobile devices without a dense dedicated
infrastructure [1]–[3]. Continuous sensing is costly and par-
ticipation is intermittent, so practical systems observe only
a sparse field and rely on completion [4]–[11]. Federated
learning trains those models without centralizing raw records
[12]–[15], including asynchronous aggregation for irregular or
stale updates [16]–[19]. The system studied here iswindowed
asynchronous: one global update per window at close, not
immediate-update FedAsync.
A less visible issue arises before aggregation:the data
that reach the server are not necessarily sampled from the
distribution that the deployed predictor is intended to serve.
For an atomic sensing targeti, a mobile client must first
have an opportunity to coveri, the target must then actu-
ally be observed, and finally the client’s local update must

contains three distinct selection layers, mobile opportunity
R, observationO, and usable updateU, whose probabili-
ties depend on mobility, sensing conditions, resource state,
network quality, deadlines, and staleness. Even when each
layer is operationally reasonable, their composition can make
the distribution of observed and successfully delivered data
differ from the public deployment target, so optimizing the
distribution induced by arriving updates can optimize the
wrong population risk.
Sparse MCS completion primarily asks how to infer missing
values from the available observations [5], [20]; asynchronous
federated completion asks how to learn effectively from irreg-
ular or stale client updates [16], [19]; and participation-aware
FL studies partial, arbitrary, or biased client participation [21]–
[28]. These directions establish that sparsity, asynchrony, and
non-uniform participation matter, but they do not resolve the
following MCS problem:how should one align asynchronous
federated data completion with a deployment target when
sensing-side availability and update-side usability jointly se-
lect the data that enter learning?Correcting only the final
client-participation distribution does not identify whether the
distortion arose from mobile opportunity, actual observation,
or usable-update censoring, and reconstructing a statistically
correct target mass does not imply that this mass is realizable
by the clients available in a finite mobile window.

Via Exposure-aware Normalization for Mobile CrowdSens-
ing. RA VEN-MCS treats deployment-target mass alignment,
rather than raw update frequency, as the reference semantics

Fig. 1, the method separates the public deployment target from
the arrival/usable-update law, then factors the opportunity,
observation, and usable-update layers under their legal pre-

not outcome or test labels). RA VEN-Design uses the lagged
target-mass opportunity ratio with Design configured on before
the window opens; RA VEN-NoDesign configures that factor
at one, retaining the arrival opportunity law. The resulting
statistical reference may still be unattainable in a particular
mobile window, so RA VEN-MCS maps the corrected reference
to a feasible aggregation decision through instantaneous target

Our main contributions are as follows.
•Deployment-target risk semantics under two-stage
non-random selection.We formulate asynchronous fed-
erated MCS completion as a deployment-target alignment
problem rather than an arrival-centered learning problem.
The formulation distinguishes the atomic deployment law
from the law induced by mobile opportunity, observation,
and usable-update selection, and defines structural mis-
match at both deployment-group and client–opportunity-
stratum levels. We prove that when the deployment and
arrival laws differ, their induced risk functionals are not
generally interchangeable. This distinction identifies the
population risk represented by selected observations and
the deployment population the model serves.
•Selection-layer-aware target reconstruction.We de-
velop an exposure-aware normalized reconstruction that
keeps the opportunity/design, observation, and usable-
update mechanisms separate instead of collapsing them
into a single missingness probability, using only legal
pre-outcome information and local H ´ajek normalization
to yield a selection-corrected reference mass for ag-
gregation. Usable-update correction supplies the third
sequential factor. Under compatible target support, se-
quential pre-outcome ignorability, and correct ideal se-
lection ratios, the ideal pooled normalized sequential
estimator with the target-mass opportunity ratio recovers
compatible target moments or target-group mass. Clip-
ping controls the finite-sample bias–variance trade-off of
the practical estimator. That reconstruction is RA VEN-
Design, the setting that turns the Design factor on; with
the factor off, RA VEN-NoDesign fixes the opportunity
factor at one. The factor is an explicit configuration that
the deployment fixes before the window opens and holds
throughout it. Both settings are reported; (42) describes
a block-level target-support check. A finite-sample cov-
erage bound is audited as an empirical diagnostic in the
supplemental material.

II. RELATEDWORK
Sparse MCS completion infers unsensed cells from a par-
tial spatiotemporal field [1]–[11], [20], [29]. Recruitment,
incentives, and privacy [30]–[33] are orthogonal and do not
formulate deployment-target risk under sequential selection.
Federated completion keeps sensing records on clients
[12]–[15]. Wanget al.[16] combine local completion with
a time-aware architecture and asynchronous aggregation for
sparse MCS, in whose spirit we study windowed rather than
immediate-update asynchrony. FedAsync and staleness-aware
variants update as models arrive [17]–[19], [34], [35]; those
works ask how to learn from the updates that participate,
whereas RA VEN-MCS models which target mass those up-
dates represent. Federated conformal prediction builds label-
set coverage under heterogeneity or label shift [36], [37];
the diagnostic Gate in Section V-E is a window-level lower
bound on realized aggregate opportunity coverageC t, not a
prediction set and not the Design rule.
Non-uniform participation is the complementary FL object.
Unified convergence under arbitrary participation, FedV ARP,
and anchor sampling address partial client presence [21]–[23],
and FedAU estimates aggregation weights from participation
history so that unknown participation does not replace the
original FL objective [24]. Correlated availability and semi-
asynchronous bias have likewise been debiased [25], [27];
agnostic FL optimizes a worst-case client mixture [26]; and
FedProx and SCAFFOLD address client drift [38], [39]. These
methods correct participation, while RA VEN-MCS addition-
ally separates sensing-side opportunity and observation from
update-side usability. FedIPW [28] is closest in the two-
stage FL vocabulary, but its factorization of enrollment and
round-level participation stays on the client roster: it does not
factor MCS opportunity or observation of sensing units, and
the aligned object is not a public deployment measure over
h(i). It is cited as the nearest enrollment–participation IPW,
not as an MCS completion baseline. FLOSS [40] reweights
aggregation for client opt-out and stragglers. FedPALS [41]
aggregates toward a known target label distribution by a
convex combination of client models. Projecting the corrected
reference ontoC r is that same matching step applied to a
deployment-group composition rather than to labels, and Sec-
tion VII-C evaluates this deployment-group projection directly.
Classical survey estimators supply the H ´ajek building blocks
[42]–[45], and importance weighting can lose effect in over-
parameterized networks [46]. A nearest-neighbor comparison
on target, selection, sensing-side correction, and finite-window
calibration is in the supplemental material.

VIII. CONCLUSION
RA VEN-MCS connects deployment-target semantics with
feasible aggregation in windowed-asynchronous sensing. The
sequential reconstruction represents the opportunity, obser-
vation, and usable-update layers explicitly, while calibration
maps the corrected reference into the mixtures available at
window closure. With Design configured on, the ideal correc-
tion under A1–A3 recovers compatible target moments and
group mass. The calibration program has a unique minimizer,
and the clipped plug-in admits a stability bound on shared
target support.
The paired experiments attribute structural alignment to
uniform aggregation, the corrected reference, and its feasible
projection. On SensorScope, the projection improves group
mismatch by+0.768percentage points over uniform weights,
and the Design factor contributes+0.922points on the same
active set. Separate mobility and observation protocols char-
acterize how these structural effects relate to deployment-
weighted prediction. Together, the formulation and measure-
ments distinguish statistical target reconstruction from finite-
window realizability and provide a basis for choosing aggre-
gation weights for a specified sensing population.
