RA VEN-MCS: Target-Mass-Calibrated
Windowed-Asynchronous Federated Data
Completion for Mobile Crowdsensing under
Two-Stage Non-Random Selection
**** and Anfeng Liu
Abstract—Mobile crowdsensing completion learns from ob-
servations selected by opportunity, observation, and update
delivery. Correcting the resulting arrival distribution can recover
a deployment-target reference, but that reference may remain
unrealizable by the clients available in a finite window. RA VEN-
MCS connects sequential reference reconstruction over atomic
sensing units to feasible calibration on the current client support,
with client weight caps and effective-support constraints. Its
aggregation program balances reference fidelity against target
composition and accumulated under-service, using selection his-
tories available before each outcome. We establish ideal target-
moment recovery under support and identification assumptions
and calibration stability at a fixed window state. Paired com-
parisons on shared client support distinguish structural gains
explained by Uniform from the increment supplied by the cor-
rected reference: on SensorScope, a feasible reference-projection
comparator reduces group mismatch by0.768percentage points
relative to Uniform. Structural alignment does not consistently
improve prediction, and projection can achieve lower group
mismatch and runtime than the full RA VEN criterion. These
findings separate the roles of population reconstruction and finite-
window calibration in federated completion.
Index Terms—Mobile crowdsensing, federated learning, data
completion, windowed asynchronous learning, non-random selec-
tion, selection bias, target-mass alignment.
I. INTRODUCTION
Mobile crowdsensing (MCS) observes sparse urban and
environmental fields, making data completion necessary [1],
[2]. Federated learning trains completion models without
centralizing sensing records [3], [4]. We study windowed
asynchronous learning: clients compute and deliver updates at
different times, while the server performs one global update
when each window closes. The challenge is to determine
which deployment population those updates represent and how
closely their aggregation can serve it.
For an atomic sensing unit, a client must have a mobile
opportunity (R), actually observe the unit (O), and deliver
a usable update before the deadline (U). These sequential
selections induce an arrival distribution that can differ from the
public deployment target. Correcting client participation alone
does not describe which sensing units entered each update. A

Existing target-aware FL already addresses population se-
lection and composition matching. FedIPW factors enrollment
and participation and calibrates to population summaries [5].
FedPALS matches a target distribution with convex aggrega-
tion and effective-sample-size regularization, including targets
outside the client convex hull [6]. The MCS question is how
to connect a reference reconstructed across sensing-unit and
update selections to a changing window’s constrained client
mixture. Inverse weighting and convex calibration supply
building blocks, but the reference and the executable aggrega-
tion decision have different meanings.
We developRA VEN-MCS(Reference Alignment Via
Exposure-aware Normalization for Mobile CrowdSensing)
around this distinction (Fig. 1). Each selection layer has its
own information boundary. Observation weights use histories
available before sensing, while usable-update weights can also
use the registered sensing state before usability is known.
Combined with a lagged opportunity ratio, these corrections
reconstruct the target mass represented by delivered updates.
The reference is not yet an executable allocation. The server
calibrates it on available clients under weight caps and
minimum effective support. The aggregation program jointly
balances reference fidelity, deployment-group matching, un-
certainty, staleness, and accumulated target debt. Debt records
under-service across windows rather than creating support
absent from the current one. Even when several weights are
feasible, clients with similar group compositions can offer little
freedom to adjust target mass.
This connection separates statistical identification from op-
erational realizability. Ideal pooled reconstruction recovers
target moments under support and identification assumptions,
whereas the implemented clipped reference includes stabiliza-
tion error. Calibration stability holds at a fixed window state,
while long-run alignment additionally requires sublinear debt
growth. Neither property establishes prediction improvement.

II. RELATEDWORK
Sparse MCS completion infers missing spatiotemporal val-
ues from partial observations [1], [2], [7]–[17]. Federated
completion keeps records on clients, while asynchronous and
staleness-aware methods address irregular update arrival [3],
[4], [18]–[25]. Their learning and timing mechanisms comple-
ment the question studied here: which deployment mass the
participating updates represent. Windowed aggregation fixes
the server decision boundary while allowing different client
start times and model versions, so update content remains
distinct from arrival time.
Participation-aware FL addresses partial client presence
through convergence analysis, update-memory correction, and
sampling [26]–[28]. FedAU estimates participation-based ag-
gregation weights [29]. Related methods address correlated
availability, semi-asynchronous bias, opt-out, and stragglers
[30]–[32]. These client-level treatments motivate separating
participation from the opportunity and observation selections
that determine each update’s sensing-unit content. RA VEN
models all three layers with distinct information histories.
FedIPW [5] is a closer reference: it factors client enrollment
and round participation, and its limited-information extension
calibrates to population summaries. Sequential weighting and
population calibration are thus established at the client level.
FedPALS [6] matches a known target label distribution through
convex aggregation, treats targets outside the client convex
hull, and trades composition error against inverse sample-
level ESS. RA VEN connects an atomic sensing-unit reference
reconstructed across sequential selections to the usable clients
in each window, under pre-observation and pre-delivery infor-
mation boundaries. It imposes hard client caps and client-level
ESS constraints on that support and tracks residual under-
service through target debt. Reference projection in client-
weight space and target matching in composition space are
related but distinct operations. The supplemental comparison
details the information and guarantee scopes. A soft sample-
size penalty and a hard client-support constraint control dif-
ferent sources of weight concentration. Classical IPW and

D. Information Boundary and Stabilization
Opportunity estimates are lagged, observation models ex-
clude current responses, and usable-update models excludeU
outcomes and future information. Current update coordinates,
norms, or directions never determine that update’s weight.
Clipping stabilizes finite samples but introduces error distinct
from estimation error. Ideal recovery requires support, correct
ratios, and sequential identification (Section VI). Inactive
clipping alone does not establish these conditions.

VIII. CONCLUSION
RA VEN-MCS connects a sensing-unit reference recon-
structed across sequential selection to the constrained client
mixtures available in an asynchronous window. Ideal mo-
ment recovery and fixed-window calibration stability address
different parts of this connection, with long-run alignment
conditional on debt growth. The experiments separate struc-
tural changes already explained by Uniform from reference
projection and independent Design effects. Simple projection
is faster and can improve group alignment over full RA VEN,
while the full criterion improves another structural endpoint.
These differences do not establish consistent prediction gains.
Target concentration, geometry, update-rule, and learning di-
agnostics show why deployment-target alignment must be
evaluated jointly with task performance and cost under the
actual protocol.
