========================= ABSTRACT =========================
Abstract—Mobile crowdsensing completion is trained on up- dates selected by opportunity, observation, and usable-update delivery. That arrival law need not match the public deployment target. RAVEN-MCS treats windowed-asynchronous federated completion as target-mass alignment. The server updates once at window close, forms a selection-corrected reference from legal pre-outcome R/O/U histories, and calibrates it inside the current feasible window. When the two laws differ, their risks are not interchangeable. The ideal sequential correction recovers compatible target moments or group mass under support and identification; it does not prove optimization of deployment- target risk. Calibration is uniquely solvable, and the clipped plug-in is a stable perturbation of that reference when target support is compatible. The Design factor is an explicit con- figuration: RAVEN-Design uses the lagged opportunity/design ratio, RAVEN-NoDesign leaves that factor at one and does not reconstruct target mass. Both settings are reported on every block rather than chosen by a data-derived rule, because they trade off across endpoints: Design is worth 1.24% of group mismatch on SensorScope at unchanged prediction, and on U-Air it costs 6.55% of RMSE while improving cross-sensor mismatch by 4.99%. A SensorScope development table under a protocol- constructed Complete-aligned selection reports RAVEN-Design as structural alignment with a descriptive +0.5% worst-group tolerance, not an RMSE win. On a T-Drive taxi replay, FedAU- Window and ObsUse-Window remain strongest, and the Design- off setting does not meet that tolerance versus those two. No ranking jointly measures R, O, and U. Index Terms—Mobile crowdsensing, federated learning, data completion, windowed asynchronous learning, non-random selec- tion, selection bias, target-mass alignment. I.
