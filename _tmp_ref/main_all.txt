RAVEN-MCS: Target-Mass-Calibrated Windowed-Asynchronous Federated Data Completion for Mobile Crowdsensing under
Two-Stage Non-Random Selection
**** and Anfeng Liu

Abstract--Mobile crowdsensing completion is trained on updates selected by opportunity, observation, and usable-update delivery. That arrival law need not match the public deployment target. RAVEN-MCS treats windowed-asynchronous federated completion as target-mass alignment. The server updates once at window close, forms a selection-corrected reference from legal pre-outcome R/O/U histories, and calibrates it inside the current feasible window. When the two laws differ, their risks are not interchangeable. The ideal sequential correction recovers compatible target moments or group mass under support and identification; it does not prove optimization of deploymenttarget risk. Calibration is uniquely solvable, and the clipped plug-in is a stable perturbation of that reference when target support is compatible. The Design factor is an explicit configuration: RAVEN-Design uses the lagged opportunity/design ratio, RAVEN-NoDesign leaves that factor at one and does not reconstruct target mass. Both settings are reported on every block rather than chosen by a data-derived rule, because they trade off across endpoints: Design is worth 1.24% of group mismatch on SensorScope at unchanged prediction, and on U-Air it costs 6.55% of RMSE while improving cross-sensor mismatch by 4.99%. A SensorScope development table under a protocolconstructed Complete-aligned selection reports RAVEN-Design as structural alignment with a descriptive +0.5% worst-group tolerance, not an RMSE win. On a T-Drive taxi replay, FedAUWindow and ObsUse-Window remain strongest, and the Designoff setting does not meet that tolerance versus those two. No ranking jointly measures R, O, and U .
Index Terms--Mobile crowdsensing, federated learning, data completion, windowed asynchronous learning, non-random selection, selection bias, target-mass alignment.
I. INTRODUCTION
Mobile crowdsensing (MCS) collects urban and environmental data from mobile devices without a dense dedicated infrastructure [1]-[3]. Continuous sensing is costly and participation is intermittent, so practical systems observe only a sparse field and rely on completion [4]-[11]. Federated learning trains those models without centralizing raw records [12]-[15], including asynchronous aggregation for irregular or stale updates [16]-[19]. The system studied here is windowed asynchronous: updates may arrive at different times inside a

A less visible issue arises before aggregation: the data that reach the server are not necessarily sampled from the distribution that the deployed predictor is intended to serve. Consider an atomic sensing target i. A mobile client must first have an opportunity to cover i; the target must then actually be observed; finally, the client's local update must complete computation and communication before the server's usableupdate deadline. Thus, the two-stage MCS process contains three distinct selection layers: mobile opportunity R, observation O, and usable update U . Their probabilities depend on mobility, sensing conditions, resource state, network quality, deadlines, and staleness. Even when each individual layer is operationally reasonable, their composition can make the distribution of observed and successfully delivered data differ from the public deployment target. Optimizing the distribution induced by arriving updates can therefore optimize the wrong population risk.
Existing work leaves an important gap at this point. Sparse MCS completion primarily asks how to infer missing values from the available observations [5], [20]; asynchronous federated completion asks how to learn effectively from irregular or stale client updates [16], [19]; and participation-aware FL studies partial, arbitrary, or biased client participation [21]-[28]. These directions establish that sparsity, asynchrony, and nonuniform participation matter, but they do not by themselves resolve the following MCS problem: how should one align asynchronous federated data completion with a deployment target when sensing-side availability and update-side usability jointly select the data that enter learning? In particular, correcting only the final client-participation distribution does not identify whether the distortion arose from mobile opportunity, actual observation, or usable-update censoring; conversely, reconstructing a statistically correct target mass does not imply that this mass is realizable by the clients available in a finite mobile window.
We develop RAVEN-MCS, short for Reference Alignment Via Exposure-aware Normalization for Mobile CrowdSensing. RAVEN-MCS treats deployment-target mass alignment, rather than raw update frequency, as the reference semantics of asynchronous federated data completion. Our main contributions are as follows.
· Deployment-target risk semantics under two-stage non-random selection. We formulate asynchronous federated MCS completion as a deployment-target alignment problem rather than an arrival-centered learning problem. The formulation distinguishes the atomic deployment law from the law induced by mobile opportunity, observation, and usable-update selection, and defines structural mismatch at both deployment-group and client-opportunitystratum levels. We prove that when the deployment and arrival laws differ, their induced risk functionals are not generally interchangeable. This provides the formal basis for asking whether a model is trained for the population it is actually intended to serve, not whether completion RMSE is optimized.
· Selection-layer-aware target reconstruction. We develop an exposure-aware normalized reconstruction that keeps the opportunity/design, observation, and usableupdate mechanisms separate instead of collapsing them into a single missingness probability. The correction

uses only legal pre-outcome information and local Ha´jek normalization, and yields a selection-corrected reference mass for aggregation. Usable-update correction is retained as a third sequential factor, not as an isolated empirical driver. Under compatible target support, sequential pre-outcome ignorability, and correct ideal selection ratios, we show that the ideal pooled normalized sequential estimator, with the target-mass opportunity ratio, recovers compatible target moments or target-group mass. That is not a proof that sequential correction optimizes deployment-target risk, nor a claim of unbiased local gradients or convergence under asynchrony. Clipping is a finite-sample stabilizer, not an unbiasedness claim. That reconstruction is RAVEN-Design, the setting in which the Design factor is on. With the factor off, the implemented configuration is RAVEN-NoDesign: the opportunity factor is one, and the result is not a targetmass reconstruction. The factor is an explicit configuration rather than a data-derived selector, and both settings are reported. A finite-sample coverage bound is audited as an empirical diagnostic in the supplemental material. · Feasibility-aware instantaneous and long-term target calibration. We show that statistically reconstructed target mass and instantaneous mobile realizability are different problems. RAVEN-MCS therefore calibrates the corrected reference inside the current feasible aggregation set while accounting for target mismatch, reference fidelity, uncertainty, and staleness, and carries unresolved group under-service through a long-term target-debt process. The resulting calibration program has a nonempty compact convex feasible set and a unique global minimizer under the stated conditions. When the active set is held fixed and target support is compatible, the implemented RAVEN-Design clipped plug-in is a Lipschitz

Target debt is an internal accounting recursion for residual group under-service, not a standalone performance contribution; empirically, instantaneous calibration and debt are not prediction drivers.
RAVEN's novelty is therefore not inverse-probability weighting or Ha´jek normalization as such. It is the combination of deployment-target semantics, selection-layer legal histories, and finite-support calibration. Isolated usable-update and debt terms are retained for sequential factorization and accounting; they are not the empirical claim.

II. RELATED WORK
Sparse MCS completion infers unsensed cells from a partial spatiotemporal field [1]-[11], [20], [29]. Recruitment, incentives, and privacy [30]-[33] are orthogonal; they do not formulate deployment-target risk under sequential selection.
Federated completion keeps sensing records on clients [12]- [15]. Wang et al. [16] combine local completion with a timeaware architecture and asynchronous aggregation for sparse MCS, in the spirit of which we study windowed rather than immediate-update asynchrony. FedAsync and staleness-aware variants update as models arrive [17]-[19], [34], [35]. Those works ask how to learn from the updates that participate. RAVEN-MCS additionally models which target mass those updates represent.
Federated conformal prediction builds label-set coverage under heterogeneity or label shift [36], [37]. The diagnostic Gate in Section V-E is a window-level lower bound on realized aggregate opportunity coverage Ct, not a prediction set and not the Design rule.
Non-uniform participation is the complementary FL object. Unified convergence under arbitrary participation, FedVARP, and anchor sampling address partial client presence [21]- [23]. FedAU estimates aggregation weights from participation

history so that unknown participation does not replace the original FL objective [24]. Correlated availability and hierarchical semi-asynchronous bias have likewise been debiased [25], [27]; agnostic FL optimizes a worst-case client mixture [26]. FedProx and SCAFFOLD address client drift [38], [39]. These methods correct participation. RAVEN-MCS additionally separates sensing-side opportunity and observation from update-side usability. Closest in the two-stage FL vocabulary is FedIPW [28], which factors enrollment then round-level participation toward a target-population mean client update. That factorization is still on the client roster: it does not factor MCS opportunity or observation of sensing units, and the aligned object is not a public deployment measure over h(i). We cite it as the nearest enrollment-participation IPW, not as an MCS completion baseline. FLOSS [40] reweights aggregation for client opt-out and stragglers. FLOSS and FedIPW are clientlevel participation corrections; the aligned object here is MCS unit-level R/O/U selection of sensing units. FedPALS [41] aggregates toward a known target label distribution under label shift. That object is class-label marginals, not MCS opportunity or observation of sensing units, and the aligned quantity is not the public deployment measure over h(i); we do not run it as a comparator. Classical survey estimators supply the Ha´jek building blocks [42]-[45]; importance weighting can lose effect in over-parameterized networks [46], which is not claimed here. III. SYSTEM MODEL AND TARGET-MASS FORMULATION
A. Windowed Asynchronous Mobile Crowdsensing
We consider a mobile crowdsensing (MCS) system with a server and a set of clients K = {1, . . . , K}. The common prediction domain is partitioned into atomic units i  I; an atomic unit may be written as i = (n, t), where n is a spatial unit and t is an absolute time index. Unit i has deployment target Yi and public deployment context Cidep. All clients contribute to a common predictor Yi = f(i; Cidep). A client-side observation of unit i is denoted by Zk,r,i when it is available to client k in server window r.
Server time is divided into aggregation windows Wr = [Tr, Tr+1) for r = 0, 1, . . .. The server keeps r fixed during Wr. Client k may start local training from a previously downloaded model k,r , where k,r  r. We use k,r  r - k,r as the model-version staleness of that attempt. Updates may arrive at different times within one window, but individual arrivals do not immediately modify the global model. The server closes the window, determines which registered attempts are usable, computes one aggregation vector, and then performs the single transition r  r+1. This windowed-asynchronous semantics is distinct from immediate-update FedAsync, in which an arrival would revise the global model before the window closes. It keeps exogenous mobile/update events independent of the method-specific aggregation weights.

B. Two-Stage, Three-Layer Non-Random Selection
The title's two-stage selection consists of a sensing-side stage and an update-side stage. The sensing-side stage itself has two layers: mobile opportunity and actual observation. The update-side stage determines whether a registered client attempt becomes usable before window closure.
For client k, window r, and unit i, let Rk,r,i  {0, 1} be the realized mobile opportunity indicator. It indicates whether (k, i) belongs to the opportunity/risk set in window r before the current sensing outcome is revealed. Conditional on Rk,r,i = 1, Ok,r,i  {0, 1} indicates whether unit i is actually observed. A client with a nonempty observed buffer registers an update attempt; the set of registered clients is Er. For k  Er, the attempted update is usable (Uk,r = 1) only if computation and transmission succeed, the update arrives by Tr+1, and its staleness satisfies k,r  Smax. Otherwise, Uk,r = 0. We first define the usable set Ur  {k  Er : Uk,r = 1}. After selection-corrected masses are formed in Section IV, zero-mass clients are removed and the actual aggregation set Ar  Ur is formed. Failures, timeouts, network interruptions, and stale arrivals remain recorded as Uk,r = 0 rather than disappearing from the selection log.
We distinguish two structural mappings: the deploymenttarget group h(i)  G and the opportunity stratum s(i)  S. The group mapping specifies which deployment mass the predictor should serve, whereas the stratum mapping pools repeatable mobile exposure patterns for selection modeling. The two mappings are different scientific objects, so h(i) = s(i) in general. Both mappings are frozen from public/trainingside information and do not use test-label values.

Every correction applied in window r is measurable before its own random outcome. Opportunity estimates use lagged opportunity history, observation propensities exclude the current sensing outcome and label, and usable-update propensities exclude the realized U outcome and future events. The server determines r without inspecting the coordinates, norm, or direction of a current random update for the purpose of assigning that update's weight. The clipping constants in (16) and (23) are finite-sample bias-variance stabilizers. Exact identification in Section VI concerns the ideal ratios, or equivalently regimes in which the stabilizing bounds are inactive on target support.

VII. EVALUATION
We evaluate RAVEN-MCS on risk interchangeability, RAVEN-Design versus the RAVEN-NoDesign setting, and component roles.
· Risk interchangeability: Does two-stage non-random selection create a deployment-target mismatch, including under real taxi opportunity?
· RAVEN-Design and RAVEN-NoDesign: On the protocol-R development table, does RAVEN-Design reduce structural mismatch, and how do the two Design settings trade off against each other and against prediction?
· Component roles: Which terms move structural diagnostics versus prediction, and when is Design useful as a reconstruction factor?
The balanced control is a no-harm check. A riskinterchangeability experiment tests whether arrival-weighted and deployment-target-weighted risks are interchangeable. A T-Drive taxi replay then checks the same four-block mismatch under real R and ranks the same family with controlled O and U . FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window remain strongest there; those rows are not mixed into Table III. the City Scanner visit-occupancy ranking table of the supplemental material ranks the same family under real visit occupancy and is likewise not mixed into Table III. the Pilot 2 measured-O ranking table of the supplemental material ranks Pilot 2 with measured O and controlled U , and is not mixed into that table either. The main comparison is a development table: it

reports both Design settings on both blocks under always-on protocol R on station traces; it is not a jointly measured mobile ranking. The supplemental material reports an intermittentoccupancy protocol table on the same station clients. A component-removal ablation drops one component at a time: on SensorScope, Design moves group while RMSEµ stays essentially flat, so group is a structural diagnostic.

2) Selection protocol and compared methods: The main comparison and ablation condition is Complete-aligned: protocol R, O, and U are all active and non-random in the same direction. On SensorScope and U-Air, protocol R is always-on. A supplemental ranking thins that R to the published T-Drive active fraction 0.425.
These layers are not measured in every block. Opportunity is real on T-Drive, from taxi trajectories, and on the two City Scanner blocks, from visit occupancy. Observation is measured on City Scanner Pilot 2 only, and usable-update delivery is measured in no block. The SensorScope, U-Air, and NSWTraffic protocol blocks are semi-synthetic. Their strata and target maps come from the frozen public data, while all three selection layers are constructed, and the tables label these layers as controls. In those blocks opportunity is always-on and its stratum is drawn from a joint opportunity law with equal client mass and a factor exp(-1.25) against UTC blocks 2 and 3. Observation is Bernoulli at rate 0.35, reduced to 0.1925 on the same two blocks. The usable-update decision is a device-tier compute and network process that disadvantages those blocks, with staleness capped at smax = 5, and each client-window draws at most four risk units. Both construction parameters are constant within a stratum rather than varying per unit: the observation rate depends only on the UTC block, which the opportunity stratum already fixes, and the usableupdate rate is drawn once per client-window and shared by every risk unit in it. The reported predictive gains describe this construction, not the range attainable when selection varies at the unit level. The compounding direction is therefore part of the construction rather than a measured property of these traces, and the structural-alignment results of Table III are conditional on it.

F. Recoverable-Gain Ceiling
Only the part of the prediction error that a selectionaware reweighting could remove in principle is reachable by any correction in this family. We measure that part by replacing the estimated propensities with their true values and reporting H = (RMSEµ(FedAvg) - RMSEµ(oracle))/RMSEµ(FedAvg) over a stress grid that varies the opportunity tilt, the tail observation scale, the model capacity and the target. The GO rule was frozen before the runs: GO if any condition reaches H  0.5% with a positive interval lower bound. It did not fire. SensorScope is conclusive at a paired-difference minimum detectable effect of 0.17-0.36 percentage points, with all six estimates inside [-0.03%, +0.08%]. U-Air was re-run at 20 seeds, 270 runs and 45 regenerated traces, where every estimate satisfies |H|  0.217% and every 95% interval contains zero; its realized detectable effect is 0.70-0.80 points, so that block excludes effects above about 0.7% and does not resolve the 0.5% bar on its own. Hardening the observation propensity to vary within a stratum and to correlate with the label raises the oracle gap by 15-21×, to 0.104% on SensorScope and 0.251% on U-Air, both inside the same bound. The ceiling is therefore a property of these endpoints rather than of a regime that was made too easy, and it bounds what any later claim about the correction family can reach on these blocks.
G. Limitations
RAVEN-Design and City Scanner report Design on. RAVEN-NoDesign reports Design off on U-Air and T-Drive (o = 1). Both settings are reported on the blocks where the trade-off is material. On Table III, SensorScope RAVENDesign and U-Air RAVEN-NoDesign stay within the descriptive +0.5% TEST tolerance versus FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window; we do not claim a Holm-significant RMSE win. Table III is a development table under always-on protocol R on station traces, not a real-mobility ranking. Table II is outside that tolerance: FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window remain strongest under real taxi R. the NSW-Traffic four-region, City Scanner visit-occupancy, and the Pilot 2 measured-O ranking table of the supplemental material are not mixed into Table III. The Design factor is an explicit configuration on each block's own tar map; neither setting is derived from a data statistic, and neither verifies positivity on target-positive pairs. A1-A2 remain support and confounding boundaries, and A6 is not verified on the released traces, so Proposition 3 is used with its clipping residual rather than with a vanishing one. The 25.5% figure is EventTrace occurrence (13040 of 51200), not the target-coverage statistic of the supplemental material. On a frozen coverage-stress oracle, pooled CERTIFIED validity is 0.959 and U-Air a = 1.00 is 0.750. Adaptive RESET/CERTIFIED switching is not proven valid, and Design is not opened from Gatet. Federated learning itself does not provide a privacy guarantee.

VIII. CONCLUSION
Windowed-asynchronous federated MCS completion should align selected mass with the public deployment target, not with the arrival law of usable updates. RAVEN-Design reconstructs compatible target mass and calibrates that reference inside the current feasible window. RAVEN-NoDesign uses  = 1 and does not reconstruct target mass on that layer. When the two laws differ, their risks are not interchangeable. The ideal sequential correction recovers compatible target moments or group mass under A1-A3; it does not prove optimization of deployment-target risk. Calibration is uniquely solvable, and the clipped plug-in is a stable perturbation of the same-window reference when target support is compatible.
On the protocol development table, SensorScope RAVENDesign reports structural alignment and a descriptive +0.5% worst-group tolerance, not an RMSE win. U-Air and T-Drive report RAVEN-NoDesign.
