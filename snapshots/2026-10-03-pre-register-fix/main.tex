\begin{abstract}
Mobile crowdsensing completion is trained on updates selected by opportunity,
observation, and usable-update delivery, and that arrival law need not match the
public deployment target.  RAVEN-MCS treats windowed-asynchronous federated
completion as target-mass alignment: the server updates once at window close,
forms a selection-corrected reference from legal pre-outcome R/O/U histories, and
calibrates it inside the current feasible window.  When the two laws differ, their
risks are not interchangeable, and the ideal sequential correction recovers
compatible target moments or group mass under support and identification without
proving optimization of deployment-target risk.  Calibration is uniquely
solvable, and the clipped plug-in is a stable perturbation of that reference when
target support is compatible.  The Design factor is an explicit configuration:
RAVEN-Design uses the lagged opportunity/design ratio, RAVEN-NoDesign leaves that
factor at one and does not reconstruct target mass, and both settings are
reported on every block rather than chosen by a data-derived rule because they
trade off across endpoints.  Design is worth $1.24\%$ of group mismatch on
SensorScope at unchanged prediction, and on U-Air it costs $6.55\%$ of RMSE while
improving cross-sensor mismatch by $4.99\%$.  A SensorScope development table
under a protocol-constructed Complete-aligned selection reports structural
alignment with a descriptive $+0.5\%$ worst-group tolerance, not an RMSE win; on
a T-Drive taxi replay FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window remain strongest, and the
Design-off setting does not meet that tolerance versus those two.  No ranking
jointly measures $R$, $O$, and $U$.
\end{abstract}

Mobile crowdsensing, federated learning, data completion, windowed asynchronous learning, non-random selection, selection bias, target-mass alignment.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Mobile crowdsensing (MCS) collects urban and environmental data from mobile
devices without a dense dedicated infrastructure
\cite{ganti2011mobile,guo2015mobile,ma2014opportunities}.
Continuous sensing is costly and participation is intermittent, so practical
systems observe only a sparse field and rely on completion
\cite{wang2023outlier,li2024robust,wang2023transformer,wang2024fewshot,wang2025evolutionary,xu2025rethinking,liu2026pyramid,zhang2026mapt}.
Federated learning trains those models without centralizing raw records
\cite{mcmahan2017communication,yang2019federated,kairouz2021advances,li2020federated},
including asynchronous aggregation for irregular or stale updates
\cite{wang2026federated,xie2019asynchronous,liu2023adaptive,sun2024staleness}.
The system studied here is \emph{windowed asynchronous}: updates may arrive at
different times inside a window, but the server applies one global update at
window close.  This is not immediate-update FedAsync.

A less visible issue arises before aggregation: \emph{the data that reach the
server are not necessarily sampled from the distribution that the deployed
predictor is intended to serve}.  For an atomic sensing target $i$, a mobile
client must first have an opportunity to cover $i$, the target must then actually
be observed, and finally the client's local update must complete computation and
communication before the server's usable-update deadline.  The two-stage MCS
process therefore contains three distinct selection layers, mobile opportunity
$R$, observation $O$, and usable update $U$, whose probabilities depend on
mobility, sensing conditions, resource state, network quality, deadlines, and
staleness.  Even when each layer is operationally reasonable, their composition
can make the distribution of observed and successfully delivered data differ
from the public deployment target, so optimizing the distribution induced by
arriving updates can optimize the \emph{wrong population risk}.

Existing work leaves an important gap at this point.  Sparse MCS completion
primarily asks how to infer missing values from the available observations
\cite{wang2023spatiotemporal,li2024robust}; asynchronous federated completion
asks how to learn effectively from irregular or stale client updates
\cite{wang2026federated,sun2024staleness}; and participation-aware FL studies
partial, arbitrary, or biased client participation
\cite{wang2022unified,jhunjhunwala2022fedvarp,wu2023anchor,wang2024lightweight,sun2025debiasing,mohri2019agnostic,chen2026fedcure,morishita2026who}.
These directions establish that sparsity, asynchrony, and non-uniform
participation matter, but they do not resolve the following MCS problem:
\emph{how should one align asynchronous federated data completion with a
deployment target when sensing-side availability and update-side usability
jointly select the data that enter learning?}  Correcting only the final
client-participation distribution does not identify whether the distortion arose
from mobile opportunity, actual observation, or usable-update censoring, and
reconstructing a statistically correct target mass does not imply that this mass
is realizable by the clients available in a finite mobile window.

We develop \textbf{RAVEN-MCS}, short for \textbf{Reference Alignment Via
Exposure-aware Normalization for Mobile CrowdSensing}.  RAVEN-MCS treats
deployment-target mass alignment, rather than raw update frequency, as the
reference semantics of asynchronous federated data completion.  As illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:raven_overview}, the method separates the public deployment target
from the arrival/usable-update law, then factors the opportunity, observation,
and usable-update layers under their legal pre-outcome information boundaries
(selection-time covariates, not outcome or test labels).  RAVEN-Design uses the
lagged target-mass opportunity ratio; when a frozen zero-target heuristic is
positive, RAVEN-NoDesign leaves that factor at one and is not a target-mass
estimator.  The resulting statistical reference may still be unattainable in a
particular mobile window, so RAVEN-MCS maps the corrected reference to a feasible
aggregation decision through instantaneous target calibration, a strongly convex
calibration program, and long-term target debt.  This separation yields three
distinct questions: \emph{what target should be served}, \emph{what target mass
the selected data represent}, and \emph{what target composition can be feasibly
realized now}.

Our main contributions are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deployment-target risk semantics under two-stage non-random
    selection.}
    We formulate asynchronous federated MCS completion as a
    deployment-target alignment problem rather than an arrival-centered
    learning problem.  The formulation distinguishes the atomic deployment law
    from the law induced by mobile opportunity, observation, and usable-update
    selection, and defines structural mismatch at both deployment-group and
    client--opportunity-stratum levels.  We prove that when the deployment and
    arrival laws differ, their induced risk functionals are not generally
    interchangeable.  This provides the formal basis for asking whether a model
    is trained for the population it is actually intended to serve, not whether
    completion RMSE is optimized.

    \item \textbf{Selection-layer-aware target reconstruction.}
    We develop an exposure-aware normalized reconstruction that keeps the
    opportunity/design, observation, and usable-update mechanisms separate
    instead of collapsing them into a single missingness probability, using only
    legal pre-outcome information and local H\'ajek normalization to yield a
    selection-corrected reference mass for aggregation.  Usable-update correction
    is retained as a third sequential factor, not as an isolated empirical
    driver.  Under compatible target support, sequential pre-outcome
    ignorability, and correct ideal selection ratios, the ideal pooled normalized
    sequential estimator with the target-mass opportunity ratio recovers
    compatible target moments or target-group mass; that is neither a proof that
    sequential correction optimizes deployment-target risk nor a claim of
    unbiased local gradients or convergence under asynchrony, and clipping is a
    finite-sample stabilizer rather than an unbiasedness claim.  That
    reconstruction is RAVEN-Design, the setting that turns the Design factor on;
    with the factor off, RAVEN-NoDesign leaves the opportunity factor at one and
    is not a target-mass reconstruction.  The factor is an explicit
    configuration that the deployment fixes before the window opens, by the
    support gate of \eqref{eq:design_gate} when it needs one setting, and both
    settings are reported.  A finite-sample coverage bound is audited as an
    empirical diagnostic in the supplemental material.

    \item \textbf{Feasibility-aware instantaneous and long-term target
    calibration.}
    We show that statistically reconstructed target mass and instantaneous
    mobile realizability are different problems.  RAVEN-MCS therefore calibrates
    the corrected reference inside the current feasible aggregation set while
    accounting for target mismatch, reference fidelity, uncertainty, and
    staleness, and carries unresolved group under-service through a long-term
    target-debt process.  The resulting program has a nonempty compact convex
    feasible set and a unique global minimizer under the stated conditions, and
    when the active set is held fixed with compatible target support the
    implemented RAVEN-Design clipped plug-in is a Lipschitz perturbation of the
    same-window ideal reference that the calibration program does not amplify.
    Target debt is an internal accounting recursion for residual group
    under-service, not a standalone performance contribution; empirically,
    instantaneous calibration and debt are neither prediction drivers nor
    structural contributors.

    \item \textbf{Attribution of the structural gain against a uniform-weight
    ruler.}
    We separate the published FedAvg-to-RAVEN structural difference, per dataset
    over 20 paired seeds, into the part that uniform server weights already close
    and the part that the corrected reference closes: $68.96\%$ versus $31.04\%$
    of a $2.09$ pp group gap on SensorScope, and $83.07\%$ versus $16.93\%$ of
    $1.47$ pp on U-Air.  Structural improvements are therefore quoted relative to
    uniform weights, and each is separated into the part carried by the projected
    reference $\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{C}_r}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r)$ and
    the part carried by the remaining terms of (P2), which is statistically
    equivalent to zero inside a pre-registered $\pm0.10$ band of the closable
    gap.  The projection is worth $+0.768$ pp over uniform weights on SensorScope
    ($[0.563,0.970]$, above the $0.5$ pp practical line) and $+0.286$ pp on
    U-Air ($[0.098,0.471]$, below it and unstable across seed halves); of the
    other components only the design factor survives, at $+0.922$ pp
    ($[0.721,1.141]$) on SensorScope, and it is not identifiable on U-Air, where
    the design-free variant does not keep the same active set.
\end{itemize}

RAVEN's novelty is therefore not inverse-probability weighting or H\'ajek
normalization as such.  It is the combination of deployment-target semantics,
selection-layer legal histories, and finite-support calibration.
Isolated usable-update and debt terms are retained for sequential
factorization and accounting; they are not the empirical claim.

We evaluate these contributions with a no-harm control, a risk-semantic block,
and a SensorScope/U-Air development table under always-on protocol $R$ on station
traces (Table~\ref{tab:main_effectiveness}).  Independent hybrid ceilings, namely
a T-Drive taxi replay under real $R$ with controlled $O$ and $U$, an NSW-Traffic
four-region protocol table, and the City Scanner {PM}$_{2.5}$ and Pilot~2 tables
of the supplemental material, are not mixed into that development table.  The
Design factor is set explicitly on each block, and both settings are reported
where the trade-off is material.  NSW-Traffic and City Scanner use locally
constructed target maps rather than a transferred SensorScope/U-Air lookup
object, and on T-Drive the Design-off setting stays outside the descriptive
$+0.5\%$ worst-group tolerance versus FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window.  A
supplemental occupancy-robustness block thins protocol $R$ on the same station
clients, and a coverage Gate is audited only as a diagnostic that does not switch
Design.


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Sparse MCS completion infers unsensed cells from a partial spatiotemporal
field
\cite{ganti2011mobile,guo2015mobile,ma2014opportunities,wang2023spatiotemporal,wang2023outlier,wang2023transformer,wang2024fewshot,li2024robust,wang2025evolutionary,xu2025rethinking,liu2026pyramid,zhang2026mapt,liu2026pricing}.
Recruitment, incentives, and privacy
\cite{liu2026multidimensional,zhang2025incentive,lu2025piece,zhang2026locally}
are orthogonal; they do not formulate deployment-target risk under sequential
selection.

Federated completion keeps sensing records on clients
\cite{mcmahan2017communication,yang2019federated,kairouz2021advances,li2020federated}.
Wang \emph{et al.}~\cite{wang2026federated} combine local completion with a
time-aware architecture and asynchronous aggregation for sparse MCS, in the
spirit of which we study windowed rather than immediate-update asynchrony.
FedAsync and staleness-aware variants update as models arrive
\cite{xie2019asynchronous,liu2023adaptive,sun2024staleness,islam2025seafl,li2026stdfl};
those works ask how to learn from the updates that participate, whereas
RAVEN-MCS models which target mass those updates represent.  Federated conformal
prediction builds label-set coverage under heterogeneity or label shift
\cite{lu2023federated,plassier2023conformal}; the diagnostic Gate in
Section~\ref{subsec:agg_certificate_alg} is instead a window-level lower bound on
realized aggregate opportunity coverage $C_t$, not a prediction set and not the
Design rule.

Non-uniform participation is the complementary FL object.  Unified convergence
under arbitrary participation, FedVARP, and anchor sampling address partial
client presence \cite{wang2022unified,jhunjhunwala2022fedvarp,wu2023anchor}, and
FedAU estimates aggregation weights from participation history so that unknown
participation does not replace the original FL objective
\cite{wang2024lightweight}.  Correlated availability and hierarchical
semi-asynchronous bias have likewise been debiased
\cite{sun2025debiasing,chen2026fedcure}; agnostic FL optimizes a worst-case
client mixture \cite{mohri2019agnostic}; and FedProx and SCAFFOLD address client
drift \cite{li2020fedprox,karimireddy2020scaffold}.  These methods correct
participation, while RAVEN-MCS additionally separates sensing-side opportunity
and observation from update-side usability.  Closest in the two-stage FL
vocabulary is FedIPW \cite{morishita2026who}, which factors enrollment then
round-level participation toward a target-population mean client update; that
factorization is still on the client roster, it does not factor MCS opportunity
or observation of sensing units, and the aligned object is not a public
deployment measure over $h(i)$.  We cite it as the nearest
enrollment--participation IPW, not as an MCS completion baseline, and likewise
do not run FLOSS~\cite{goetze2025floss}, which reweights aggregation for client
opt-out and stragglers, or FedPALS~\cite{listozec2024overcoming}, which
aggregates toward a known target \emph{label} distribution under label shift.
Classical survey estimators supply the H\'ajek building blocks
\cite{horvitz1952generalization,hajek1971comment,shimodaira2000covariate,robins1994estimation},
and importance weighting can lose effect in over-parameterized networks
\cite{byrd2019importance}, which is not claimed here.  A nearest-neighbor
comparison on target, selection, sensing-side correction, and finite-window
calibration is in the supplemental material.


\section{System Model and Target-Mass Formulation}
\label{sec:system_model}

\subsection{Windowed Asynchronous Mobile Crowdsensing}
\label{subsec:windowed_mcs}

We consider a mobile crowdsensing (MCS) system with a server and clients
$\mathcal{K}=\{1,\ldots,K\}$.  The prediction domain is partitioned into atomic
units $i=(n,t)\in\mathcal{I}$, where $n$ is a spatial unit and $t$ an absolute
time index; each unit carries a target value
$Y_i\in\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}$, revealed only if observed, and a
label-free deployment context $C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}$.  All clients contribute to
one completion predictor $f_{\theta}$ with parameters
$\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}$, returning
$\widehat Y_i=f_{\theta}(i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}})\in\mathcal{Y}$ and updated by
local training and aggregation.  A client-side observation of unit $i$ is
denoted by $Z_{k,r,i}$ when available to client $k$ in server window $r$.

Server time is divided into aggregation windows
$\mathcal{W}_r=[T_r,T_{r+1})$, during which the server keeps $\theta_r$ fixed.
Client $k$ may start local training from a previously downloaded model
$\theta_{\sigma_{k,r}}$ with $\sigma_{k,r}\le r$, and
$\tau_{k,r}\triangleq r-\sigma_{k,r}$ is the model-version staleness of that
attempt.  Updates may arrive at different times, but individual arrivals do not
modify the global model: the server closes the window, determines which
registered attempts are usable, computes one aggregation vector, and performs the
single transition $\theta_r\rightarrow\theta_{r+1}$.  This
\emph{windowed-asynchronous} semantics is distinct from immediate-update
FedAsync, in which an arrival would revise the global model before closure, and
it keeps exogenous mobile/update events independent of the method-specific
aggregation weights.

\subsection{Two-Stage, Three-Layer Non-Random Selection}
\label{subsec:selection_process}

The title's \emph{two-stage selection} consists of a sensing-side stage with two
layers, mobile opportunity and actual observation, and an update-side stage that
determines whether a registered client attempt becomes usable before window
closure.

For client $k$, window $r$, and unit $i$, let
$R_{k,r,i}\in\{0,1\}$ be the realized mobile opportunity indicator, which states
whether $(k,i)$ belongs to the opportunity/risk set in window $r$ before the
current sensing outcome is revealed.  Conditional on $R_{k,r,i}=1$,
$O_{k,r,i}\in\{0,1\}$ indicates whether unit $i$ is actually observed.  A client
with a nonempty observed buffer registers an update attempt, and the set of
registered clients is $\mathcal{E}_r$.  For $k\in\mathcal{E}_r$ the attempted
update is usable ($U_{k,r}=1$) only if computation and transmission succeed, the
update arrives by $T_{r+1}$, and its staleness satisfies
$\tau_{k,r}\le S_{\max}$, the block's frozen maximum admissible model-version
staleness; otherwise $U_{k,r}=0$.  We first define the usable set
$\mathcal{U}_r\triangleq\{k\in\mathcal{E}_r:U_{k,r}=1\}$; after
selection-corrected masses are formed in Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction},
zero-mass clients are removed and the actual aggregation set
$\mathcal{A}_r\subseteq\mathcal{U}_r$ is formed.  Failures, timeouts, network
interruptions, and stale arrivals remain recorded as $U_{k,r}=0$ rather than
disappearing from the selection log.

We distinguish two structural mappings from units to finite label-free index
sets: the \emph{deployment-target group} $h:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{G}$ and the
\emph{opportunity stratum} $s:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{S}$, with $\mathcal{G}$ and
$\mathcal{S}$ the finite sets of deployment groups and opportunity strata.  The
group mapping specifies which deployment mass the predictor should serve, whereas
the stratum mapping pools repeatable mobile exposure patterns for selection
modeling, so the two are different scientific objects and $h(i)\neq s(i)$ in
general.  Both are frozen from public/training-side information and do not use
test-label values.

For theoretical bookkeeping, let the normalized opportunity-event law in
window $r$ be
\begin{equation}
 P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)
 =
 \pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s(i),r}
 \nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s(i)),
 \qquad
 \sum_{k,i}P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)=1,
 \label{eq:opp_event_law}
\end{equation}
where $\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}$ is the client--stratum opportunity mass and
$\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s)$ is the conditional atomic opportunity law.
The realized risk-set indicators $R_{k,r,i}$ are the implementation-level
bookkeeping of the opportunity events induced by this mobile exposure process;
they are not assumed to be mutually independent Bernoulli draws.

The subsequent selection probabilities are conditional probabilities under the
same selection process, with $\Pr\{\cdot\}$ that probability.
\begin{align}
 p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}
 &\triangleq
 \Pr\!\left(
 O_{k,r,i}=1
 \mid
 R_{k,r,i}=1,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}
 \right),
 \label{eq:pobs_def}\\
 q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}
 &\triangleq
 \Pr\!\left(
 U_{k,r}=1
 \mid
 k\in\mathcal{E}_r,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}
 \right).
 \label{eq:quse_def}
\end{align}
The observation history $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$ contains
only information available before $O_{k,r,i}$ is realized.  The
usable-update history $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ is measured
after the sensing-side stage but before $U_{k,r}$ is realized.  It may
therefore contain the already observed registration state, but not the
realized computation/transmission outcomes, future events, or the
current update vector used for aggregation.  This sequential
information boundary is part of the statistical model.

\subsection{Public Deployment Target and Compatible Target Measures}
\label{subsec:target_measures}

We begin with a public, label-independent atomic deployment target over the
deployment domain $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dep}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}$,
\begin{equation}
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}\ge0,
    \qquad
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dep}}}
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}=1.
    \label{eq:atomic_target}
\end{equation}
Its deployment-group marginal is
$\mu_g=\sum_{i:h(i)=g}\varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}$, so
$\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{G}|-1}$.
These quantities define the application-level target served by the deployed
predictor.  Opportunity correction uses a compatible client--stratum target
$\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}$ built from the stratum mass of $s(i)$, a frozen
public client share, and the conditional atomic law on $s(i)$
(supplemental material).  Deployment groups $h(i)$ and opportunity strata
$s(i)$ need not coincide.  Compatibility requires
\begin{equation}
    \sum_k\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}
    =
    \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}},
    \qquad
    \sum_{k,s}\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}=1.
    \label{eq:target_compatibility}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:target_compatibility} is the bridge between the
deployment-risk target and the client--stratum target used for Design
and coverage.  Define $I_{\mathrm{tar}}=\{(k,s):\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}>0\}$.
The unit lookup used by the target-coverage statistic of the supplemental
material, and the pair opportunity mass used by
$C_t$, are constructed in the supplemental material.
All target quantities are frozen before test-label evaluation.

\subsection{Aggregate Coverage Certification Object}
\label{subsec:agg_coverage_object}

Realized aggregate coverage is
\begin{equation}
 C_t
 =
 \sum_{(k,s)\in I_{\mathrm{tar}}}
 \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}
 \,
 \mathbf{1}\!\left\{\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,t}\ge\pi_{\mathrm{safe}}\right\},
 \label{eq:agg_Ct}
\end{equation}
where $\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,t}$ is the lagged opportunity mass of
that pair, $\pi_{\mathrm{safe}}$ is a frozen threshold, and
$\mathbf{1}\{\cdot\}$ is the indicator of the event in braces.
A diagnostic Gate on a lower bound of $C_t$ does not switch Design.

\subsection{Deployment-Target Risk Versus Arrival Risk}
\label{subsec:target_risk}

For the squared loss
$\ell(\widehat Y,Y)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\widehat Y-Y)^2$, the deployment-target risk is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{R}_{\mu}(\theta)
 =
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dep}}}
 \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}
 \ell\!\left(
 f_{\theta}(i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}),Y_i
 \right).
 \label{eq:target_risk}
\end{equation}
By contrast, the data reaching learning are distributed according to the
opportunity, observation, and usable-update mechanisms.

For evaluation, quantities without a window index denote the frozen
evaluation-period marginals of their corresponding pre-outcome mechanisms.
Under \eqref{eq:opp_event_law}--\eqref{eq:quse_def}, the joint sequential
probability that client $k$ contributes unit $i$ to learning is
$P^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)\,p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,i}\,q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k}$, and the
label-free atomic arrival intensity is the corresponding sum, evaluated by the
plug-in
\begin{equation}
 \widehat r_i^{\mathrm{arr}}
 =
 \sum_k
 \widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s(i)}
 \widehat\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k}(i\mid s(i))
 \widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,i}
 \widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,s(i)},
 \label{eq:arrival_intensity}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,s}$ is the evaluation-period marginal of the
legal pre-$U$ usable-update model over registered attempts associated with
stratum $s$.  Normalize
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\rho_i^{\mathrm{arr}}
 =
 \frac{\widehat r_i^{\mathrm{arr}}}
 {\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dep}}}\widehat r_j^{\mathrm{arr}}},
 \label{eq:arrival_distribution}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{R}_{\rho}(\theta)
 =
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dep}}}
 \widetilde\rho_i^{\mathrm{arr}}
 \ell\!\left(
 f_{\theta}(i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}}),Y_i
 \right).
 \label{eq:arrival_risk}
\end{equation}
The two risks answer different questions.  The deployment-target risk
$\mathcal{R}_{\mu}$ measures how accurately the predictor serves the deployment
population it is intended for, and the arrival risk $\mathcal{R}_{\rho}$ measures
how accurately it fits the updates that actually reach training; the loss and the
predictor are the same in both, and only the weighting law differs.  Since
opportunity, observation, and usable-update selection are non-random, those two
populations need not coincide, so accuracy on the arriving updates need not
transfer to the deployment population and RAVEN-MCS treats deployment-target mass
alignment, rather than usable-update frequency, as the reference semantics of the
learning problem.

\subsection{Structural Alignment Objective}
\label{subsec:design_objective}

Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} will construct two corrected within-client
compositions: a deployment-group composition
$\mathbf{c}^{g}_{k,r}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{G}|-1}$ and an opportunity-stratum
composition $\mathbf{c}^{s}_{k,r}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}$.  For the final
aggregation set $\mathcal{A}_r$, define
$M_r=[\mathbf{c}^{g}_{k,r}]_{k\in\mathcal{A}_r}$.  Given feasible aggregation
weights $\boldsymbol\alpha_r\in\mathcal{C}_r$, the effective deployment-group
composition in an active window is $\boldsymbol\omega_r=M_r\boldsymbol\alpha_r$;
the feasible set $\mathcal{C}_r$ is \eqref{eq:Cr}, and $\boldsymbol\alpha_r$ is
determined by the calibration program (P2) of Section~\ref{subsec:P2} rather than
supplied as an input.

Let $\eta_r>0$ denote the server step size and define
$I_r=\mathbf{1}\{|\mathcal{A}_r|>0\}$ and
$S_R=\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\eta_r I_r$.
For $S_R>0$,
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\boldsymbol\omega}_R
    =
    \frac{1}{S_R}
    \sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
    \eta_r I_r\boldsymbol\omega_r.
    \label{eq:omega_bar}
\end{equation}
We measure the group-level structural mismatch
$\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}(R)=\left\|
\overline{\boldsymbol\omega}_R-\boldsymbol\mu
\right\|_1$,
the $\ell_1$ distance between the time-averaged effective deployment-group
composition and the deployment target.  Both sides are probability vectors
over $\mathcal{G}$, so $0\le\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}\le2$.  Both structural
endpoints are quoted in percentage points of target mass,
$\mathrm{pp}=\Delta/2\times100$.  Structural contrasts are reported relative to
uniform server weights $\alpha_{k,r}=1/|\mathcal{A}_r|$ and, where they turn on
the reference, in units of the closable gap between uniform weighting and the
projected reference, at the same active set, the same $M_r$, the same
$\mathcal{C}_r$, and with all windows known in advance; the pre-registered
equivalence band is $\pm0.10$ in those units.

For client--stratum structure, extend $\alpha_{k,r}=0$ to
$k\notin\mathcal{A}_r$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Pi_{k,s}(R)
 =
 \frac{1}{S_R}
 \sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
 \eta_r I_r\,
 \alpha_{k,r}\,
 c^{s}_{k,r,s}.
 \label{eq:Pi_hat_ks}
\end{equation}
Because each active-window $\boldsymbol\alpha_r$ and each
$\mathbf{c}^{s}_{k,r}$ are probability vectors,
$\sum_{k,s}\widehat\Pi_{k,s}(R)=1$.  We therefore measure
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{c\text{-}s}(R)
 =
 \sum_{k,s}
 \left|
 \widehat\Pi_{k,s}(R)-\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}
 \right|.
 \label{eq:delta_cs}
\end{equation}
The overall design goal is to keep $\mathcal{R}_{\mu}$ low while
reducing $\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$ and $\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$ under
time-varying mobile feasibility and legal pre-outcome information.


\section{Selection-Layer-Aware Target Reconstruction}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

RAVEN-MCS (Reference Alignment Via Exposure-aware Normalization) first forms a
selection-corrected reference for the mass the usable data represent.
Opportunity, observation, and usable-update selection are kept separate
because they have different conditioning sets and therefore cannot be
replaced by a single generic missingness probability.

\subsection{Opportunity/Design Correction}
\label{subsec:design_correction}

For a target-supported client--atomic pair, the ideal opportunity/design ratio is
\begin{equation}
 \zeta^{o}_{k,r,i}
 =
 \frac{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s(i)}}
      {\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s(i),r}}
 \frac{\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}_{i\mid s(i)}}
      {\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s(i))}
 =
 \frac{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}}
      {P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k,i)}.
 \label{eq:design_ratio_general}
\end{equation}
Under within-stratum exchangeability,
$\nu^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,r}(i\mid s)=\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}_{i\mid s}$, this reduces to
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\zeta^{o}_{k,r,s}
 =
 \frac{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}}
 {\max\{\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r},\pi_{\min}\}}.
 \label{eq:design_ratio_simple}
\end{equation}
The opportunity estimate is lagged: current-window outcomes cannot alter the
weight applied to that same window.  One admissible implementation uses
\begin{align*}
 C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}
 &=
 \rho_{\mathrm{opp}}C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r-1}
 +N^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r-1},\\
 \widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}
 &=
 \frac{C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,s,r}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opp}}}
 {\sum_{j,u}
 (C^{\mathrm{opp}}_{j,u,r}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opp}})}.
\end{align*}
Other pre-outcome estimators are compatible with the framework if they preserve
the same support and information boundary.

The Design factor $D_t\in\{\mathrm{on},\mathrm{off}\}$ of
\eqref{eq:design_factor} applies \eqref{eq:design_ratio_simple} when it is on,
which is RAVEN-Design; with the factor off,
Algorithm~\ref{alg:window_close} runs RAVEN-NoDesign and sets
$\widehat\zeta^{o}_{k,r,i}=1$ on observed pairs.  The factor is a configuration of
the deployment, supplied before the window opens rather than computed from the
data, the same two settings are reported on every block, and the off setting is
an uncorrected opportunity factor that neither replaces $\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}$ by
$\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}$ nor reconstructs target mass on that layer.

\subsection{Observation Correction and Local H\'ajek Normalization}
\label{subsec:observation_hajek}

The ideal first-stage weight for a realized opportunity is
$a^\star_{k,r,i}=\zeta^{o}_{k,r,i}/p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i}$.
The implemented plug-in weight is stabilized as
\begin{equation}
 a_{k,r,i}
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 a_{\max},
 \frac{\widehat\zeta^{o}_{k,r,i}}
 {\max\{\widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k,r,i},p_{\min}\}}
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:a_stabilized}
\end{equation}
Define the corrected total mass
\begin{equation}
    m_{k,r}
    =
    \sum_i
    R_{k,r,i}O_{k,r,i}a_{k,r,i},
    \label{eq:m_total}
\end{equation}
the corrected deployment-group mass
\begin{equation}
 m^{g}_{k,r,g}
 =
 \sum_i
 R_{k,r,i}O_{k,r,i}a_{k,r,i}
 \mathbf{1}\{h(i)=g\},
 \label{eq:m_group}
\end{equation}
and the corrected opportunity-stratum mass
\begin{equation}
 m^{s}_{k,r,s}
 =
 \sum_i
 R_{k,r,i}O_{k,r,i}a_{k,r,i}
 \mathbf{1}\{s(i)=s\}.
 \label{eq:m_stratum}
\end{equation}
RAVEN aggregates only target-relevant usable clients:
$\mathcal{A}_r\triangleq\{k\in\mathcal{U}_r:m_{k,r}>0\}$.
Every $k\in\mathcal{A}_r$ has well-defined normalized record weights
$\overline a_{k,r,i}=\frac{R_{k,r,i}O_{k,r,i}a_{k,r,i}}{m_{k,r}}$.
Its deployment-group composition is
$c^{g}_{k,r,g}=\frac{m^{g}_{k,r,g}}{m_{k,r}}$, and its
opportunity-stratum composition is
$c^{s}_{k,r,s}=\frac{m^{s}_{k,r,s}}{m_{k,r}}$.
Both composition vectors sum to one for $k\in\mathcal{A}_r$.

The local effective sample size is
\begin{equation}
 n^{\mathrm{eff}}_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{m_{k,r}^2}
 {\sum_i
  (R_{k,r,i}O_{k,r,i}a_{k,r,i})^2}
 =
 \frac{1}{\sum_i\overline a_{k,r,i}^2}.
 \label{eq:neff}
\end{equation}
It measures weight concentration and is deliberately distinct from the
target-equivalent corrected mass $m_{k,r}$.

The local completion objective is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F^{H}_{k,r}(\theta)
 =
 \sum_i
 \overline a_{k,r,i}
 \frac{1}{2}
 \left(
 f_{\theta}(i;C_i^{\mathrm{dep}})
 -
 Z_{k,r,i}
 \right)^2.
 \label{eq:local_hajek_objective}
\end{equation}
Starting from $\theta_{\sigma_{k,r}}$, client $k$ performs
$E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ local steps of $\ell$ with step size $\gamma_r>0$, reaching
$\theta^{(E_{\mathrm{loc}})}_{k,r}$.  Its normalized model
displacement is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{u}_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{
 \theta_{\sigma_{k,r}}
 -
 \theta^{(E_{\mathrm{loc}})}_{k,r}
 }{\gamma_r E_{\mathrm{loc}}}.
 \label{eq:normalized_update}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Usable-Update Correction and Reference Aggregation Mass}
\label{subsec:usable_correction}

For a registered attempt, $\widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r}$ is estimated only
from variables measurable before $U_{k,r}$ is realized.  For
$k\in\mathcal{A}_r$, define
\begin{equation}
 d_{k,r}
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 d_{\max},
 \frac{1}
 {\max\{\widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k,r},q_{\min}\}}
 \right\},
 \label{eq:d_use}
\end{equation}
and $b_{k,r}=m_{k,r}d_{k,r}$.
The selection-corrected reference aggregation vector is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\beta_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{b_{k,r}}
 {\sum_{j\in\mathcal{A}_r}b_{j,r}},
 \qquad
 k\in\mathcal{A}_r.
 \label{eq:beta_ref}
\end{equation}
Thus $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ is a probability vector on
$\mathcal{A}_r$.  The ideal untruncated factorization of
$\beta^\star\overline a^\star$ is in the supplemental material; the implemented
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ is a stabilized plug-in and is not claimed to
preserve exact unbiasedness when clipping is active.  The corrected
deployment-group composition represented by the usable data before server
calibration is $\boldsymbol\omega_r^{\mathrm{ref}}=M_r\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$,
which a finite mobile window need not make equal to $\boldsymbol\mu$, since the
current feasible client set can lack target support.

\subsection{Information Boundary and Stabilization}
\label{subsec:information_boundary}

Every correction applied in window $r$ is measurable before its own random
outcome.  Opportunity estimates use lagged opportunity history,
observation propensities exclude the current sensing outcome and label,
and usable-update propensities exclude the realized $U$ outcome and
future events.  The server determines $\boldsymbol\alpha_r$ without
inspecting the coordinates, norm, or direction of a current random
update for the purpose of assigning that update's weight.  The clipping
constants in \eqref{eq:a_stabilized} and \eqref{eq:d_use} are
finite-sample bias--variance stabilizers.  Exact identification in
Section~\ref{sec:theory} concerns the ideal ratios, or equivalently
regimes in which the stabilizing bounds are inactive on target support.

\subsection{Why Pairwise Positive Lower Bounds Are Not the Formal Certificate}
\label{subsec:pairwise_barrier}

A deterministic pre-window pairwise lower bound is structurally vacuous under
hidden zero-opportunity events by the positive pairwise lower-bound barrier
proposition in the supplemental material, whose availability-induced barrier
corollary places the frozen stress levels $a\in\{0.80,0.60,0.40,0.20\}$ in that
region.  Pairwise positive LCBs are not the aggregate object \eqref{eq:agg_Ct}
and do not switch Design.


\section{Feasibility-Aware Target Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

Selection reconstruction determines what the usable data statistically
represent.  It does not guarantee that the intended deployment target is
instantaneously realizable from the currently available clients.  RAVEN
therefore treats $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ as a corrected reference and
calibrates it within the support feasible at window closure.

\subsection{Feasible Aggregation Set}
\label{subsec:feasible_set}

Let $K_r=|\mathcal{A}_r|$ in an active window and retain
$M_r=[\mathbf{c}^{g}_{k,r}]_{k\in\mathcal{A}_r}$.  The server chooses
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r\in\mathbb{R}^{K_r}$ from
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{C}_r
 =
 \left\{
 \boldsymbol\alpha:
 \mathbf{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol\alpha=1,\;
 0\le\alpha_k\le\overline\alpha_r,\;
 \|\boldsymbol\alpha\|_2^2\le\frac{1}{\overline E_r}
 \right\},
 \label{eq:Cr}
\end{equation}
where the coordinate cap $\overline\alpha_r$ and $\ell_2$ support bound
$\overline E_r$ are frozen functions of $K_r$, the effective-support cap of the
supplemental material, with $0<\alpha_{\max}\le1$ and $E_{\min}\ge1$ as frozen
design parameters.  The cap controls single-client dominance and the $\ell_2$
constraint imposes a minimum effective aggregation support; the uniform vector
$\mathbf{1}/K_r$ belongs to $\mathcal{C}_r$, so every active window has at least
one feasible aggregation vector.

RAVEN also uses pre-aggregation uncertainty only.  A lagged variance proxy is
\begin{equation}
 v_{k,r}
 =
 \frac{
 \overline S_{k,r^-}^{\,2}
 }{
 \max\{n^{\mathrm{eff}}_{k,r},1\}
 }
 +
 v_{\mathrm{floor}},
 \qquad
 V_r=\operatorname{diag}(v_{k,r})\succeq0,
 \label{eq:variance_proxy}
\end{equation}
where $\overline S_{k,r^-}^{\,2}$ is available before the current random update
is weighted.  Let $\overline{\boldsymbol\tau}_r\ge0$ denote the frozen
pre-aggregation staleness vector.

\subsection{Long-Term Target Debt}
\label{subsec:debt}

Finite-window feasibility can make
$M_r\boldsymbol\alpha=\boldsymbol\mu$ impossible.  RAVEN maintains a
nonnegative target-debt vector
$\mathbf{Q}_r\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{G}|}_+$, initialized by
$\mathbf{Q}_0=\mathbf{0}$, updated in an active window by
$\mathbf{Q}_{r+1}=\left[\mathbf{Q}_r+\eta_r(\boldsymbol\mu-\boldsymbol\omega_r)\right]_+$
with $\boldsymbol\omega_r=M_r\boldsymbol\alpha_r$, and left unchanged in an
inactive one.  A positive $Q_{r,g}$ records accumulated under-service of
deployment group $g$ without pretending that unavailable target mass can be
synthesized in the current window.  For every prefix with $S_R>0$, the recursion
implies the deterministic accounting relation
\begin{equation}
 \left\|
 \overline{\boldsymbol\omega}_R-\boldsymbol\mu
 \right\|_1
 \le
 \frac{
 2\|\mathbf{Q}_R\|_1
 }{S_R}.
 \label{eq:debt_bound}
\end{equation}
The full derivation is in the target-debt accounting proof of the supplemental
material.

\subsection{Feasibility-Aware Calibration Program}
\label{subsec:P2}

For every active window, RAVEN solves
$\text{(P2)}\ \min_{\boldsymbol\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_r}J_r(\boldsymbol\alpha)$.
The objective is
\begin{align}
 J_r(\boldsymbol\alpha)
 ={}&-\mathbf{Q}_r^{\top}M_r\boldsymbol\alpha
 +\frac{\lambda_g}{2}
 \|M_r\boldsymbol\alpha-\boldsymbol\mu\|_2^2
 \nonumber\\
 &+\frac{\lambda_{\beta}}{2}
 \|\boldsymbol\alpha-\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r\|_2^2
 \nonumber\\
 &+\frac{\lambda_v}{2}
 \boldsymbol\alpha^{\top}V_r\boldsymbol\alpha
 +\lambda_s\overline{\boldsymbol\tau}_r^{\top}\boldsymbol\alpha.
\label{eq:P2_objective}
\end{align}
The debt term prioritizes accumulated under-served groups; the group,
reference, variance, and staleness terms are coordinated terms of one
program.  $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ is the selection-corrected
reference; $\boldsymbol\alpha_r$ is the feasible aggregation decision.

\subsection{Global Update and Window Logic}
\label{subsec:global_update}

Let $\boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star$ denote the solution of (P2).  The server update
is
\begin{equation}
 \theta_{r+1}
 =
 \theta_r
 -
 \eta_r
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}_r}
 \alpha^\star_{k,r}\mathbf{u}_{k,r}.
 \label{eq:server_update}
\end{equation}
After aggregation, the server updates the debt via
$\mathbf{Q}_{r+1}=\left[\mathbf{Q}_r+\eta_r(\boldsymbol\mu-\boldsymbol\omega_r)\right]_+$,
where $\boldsymbol\omega_r=M_r\boldsymbol\alpha_r$, and advances lagged
selection and variance states.  If $\mathcal{A}_r=\varnothing$, the
window is inactive: $\theta_{r+1}=\theta_r$, $\mathbf{Q}_{r+1}=\mathbf{Q}_r$,
and the window does not enter $S_R$.  Algorithm~\ref{alg:window_close}
records this order: reconstruct $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$, solve (P2) for
$\boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star$, then aggregate and update debt.

\subsection{Finite-Sample Aggregate Coverage Certificate}
\label{subsec:agg_certificate_alg}

The Design factor $\eqref{eq:design_factor}$ is configured on or off.  On, it
is RAVEN-Design and uses lagged opportunity ratios; off, it is RAVEN-NoDesign
and uses the uncorrected factor $1$.  A diagnostic Gate, detailed as the
aggregate coverage certificate algorithm of the supplemental material, uses only
pre-window information $F_{t-}$ with
\begin{equation}
 k_n=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil,
 \qquad
 \alpha=0.05,
 \label{eq:agg_kn}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 C^{\mathrm{LCB}}_t=\max\{0,\widehat C_t-q_t\},
 \label{eq:agg_CLCB}
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{Gate}_t=\mathbf{1}\{C^{\mathrm{LCB}}_t\ge\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}\}$ with
$\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}=0.05$.  WARMUP and RESET force the Gate off.  It does
not switch Design.

Let $D_t\in\{\mathrm{on},\mathrm{off}\}$ be the Design factor of window $t$:
a configuration of the deployment, fixed before the window opens and recorded
with it.  The implemented opportunity/design factor is
\begin{equation}
\widehat\zeta^{o}_{k,t,i}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s(i)}}
{\max\{\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,t,s(i)},\pi_{\min}\}}, & D_t=\mathrm{on},\\[1.5mm]
1, & D_t=\mathrm{off}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:design_factor}
\end{equation}
With $D_t=\mathrm{on}$ this is the lagged ratio
\eqref{eq:design_ratio_simple}, the setting called RAVEN-Design; with
$D_t=\mathrm{off}$ it is the uncorrected factor one, called RAVEN-NoDesign,
and the result is not target-mass reconstruction.
\eqref{eq:design_factor} is the only place the two settings differ: the
observation, usable-update, calibration, and debt steps are identical.
We report both settings rather than a single selected one, because the choice
trades off across endpoints; a deployment that needs one setting fixes it by the
support gate of \eqref{eq:design_gate}, which reads only public pre-window
information and states a stopping condition rather than a preference between
endpoints.  The supplemental material reports the share of training risk units
whose attached client--stratum carries zero public target mass, the input of that
gate and nothing more.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Window-close RAVEN-MCS with the Design factor as an explicit configuration}
\label{alg:window_close}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE window $t$; recorded $R_{k,t,i}$, $O_{k,t,i}$, $U_{k,t}$;
public $\boldsymbol\mu$; debt $\mathbf{Q}_t$; model $\theta_t$;
frozen public $\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}$; Design factor $D_t\in\{\mathrm{on},\mathrm{off}\}$
\ENSURE $\theta_{t+1}$, $\mathbf{Q}_{t+1}$
\STATE Form $\mathcal{E}_t$ and
$\mathcal{U}_t=\{k\in\mathcal{E}_t:U_{k,t}=1\}$.
\IF{$D_t=\mathrm{on}$}
\STATE Compute lagged opportunity/design ratios
$\widehat\zeta^{o}_{k,t,i}$ \COMMENT{RAVEN-Design}
\ELSE
\STATE Set $\widehat\zeta^{o}_{k,t,i}\leftarrow 1$ on observed pairs \COMMENT{RAVEN-NoDesign}
\ENDIF
\STATE Compute observation weights $a_{k,t,i}$ and masses $m_{k,t}$;
set $\mathcal{A}_t=\{k\in\mathcal{U}_t:m_{k,t}>0\}$.
\IF{$\mathcal{A}_t=\varnothing$}
\STATE Set $\theta_{t+1}\leftarrow\theta_t$,
$\mathbf{Q}_{t+1}\leftarrow\mathbf{Q}_t$, and stop.
\ELSE
\STATE Compute usable-update factors $d_{k,t}$ and reference
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_t$.
\STATE Form $\mathcal{C}_t$ and lagged $V_t$,
$\overline{\boldsymbol\tau}_t$; solve (P2) for
$\boldsymbol\alpha_t^\star$.
\STATE $\theta_{t+1}\leftarrow\theta_t
-\eta_t\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}_t}\alpha^\star_{k,t}\mathbf{u}_{k,t}$.
\STATE $\boldsymbol\omega_t\leftarrow M_t\boldsymbol\alpha_t^\star$,
$\mathbf{Q}_{t+1}\leftarrow
[\mathbf{Q}_t+\eta_t(\boldsymbol\mu-\boldsymbol\omega_t)]_+$.
\STATE Advance lagged selection and variance states.
\ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We state the results used by the mainline.  Proposition~\ref{prop:reconstruction}
recovers compatible target moments or group mass under A1--A3 for the ideal
Design-on sequential estimator; it proves neither optimization of
deployment-target risk nor unbiased local gradients nor convergence under
asynchrony.

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{subsec:assumptions}

\noindent\textbf{A1 (Compatible target support and positivity).}
Public targets satisfy \eqref{eq:atomic_target} and
\eqref{eq:target_compatibility}.  Inverse factors are applied only where
$P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}$, $p^{\mathrm{obs}}$, and $q^{\mathrm{use}}$ are
strictly positive on target-supported events.

\noindent\textbf{A2 (Sequential pre-outcome ignorability).}
Conditional on the legal history at each stage, $O$ and $U$ are
conditionally mean-ignorable for the reconstructed moment.  The $O$ model
uses only $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{obs}}$; the $U$ model may use the realized
sensing-side registration state, but not the future $U$ outcome
(display in the supplemental material).

\noindent\textbf{A3 (Correct ideal ratios and cluster-level regularity).}
Ideal ratios equal their population counterparts, or stabilizing bounds are
inactive on relevant support.  As $N_R^{\mathrm{opp}}\rightarrow\infty$,
weighted cluster sums obey a weak law and the H\'ajek denominator stays
strictly positive.

\noindent\textbf{A4 (Convex calibration).}
In every active window, $K_r\ge1$, $0<\alpha_{\max}\le1$, $E_{\min}\ge1$,
$V_r\succeq0$, $\lambda_{\beta}>0$, and
$\lambda_g,\lambda_v,\lambda_s\ge0$.  The feasible set is \eqref{eq:Cr}.

\noindent\textbf{A5 (Positivity and shared support).}
Every event that enters the estimator, i.e.\ each target-supported event of
A1 that belongs to the realized active set $\mathcal{A}_r$, has strictly
positive opportunity, observation, and usable-update margins, so that the
three inversions in \eqref{eq:chi_event} are well defined there.  The ideal and
the practical reference share $\mathcal{A}_r$, $M_r$, and $\mathcal{C}_r$,
and the unnormalized H\'ajek mass obeys
$\mathbf{1}^{\top}b\ge B_{\min}>0$.  The shared-support clause of A5 fails
if Design changes $\widehat\zeta$ on $\{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}=0\}$, which is why
RAVEN-NoDesign uses $\widehat\zeta=1$.

\noindent\textbf{A6a (Inactive stabilization at the true margins).}
On A5's active support the true margins clear every stabilizer of
\eqref{eq:design_ratio_simple}, \eqref{eq:a_stabilized}, and \eqref{eq:d_use}:
$\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}\ge\pi_{\min}$, $p^{\mathrm{obs}}\ge p_{\min}$,
$\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}/(\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}p^{\mathrm{obs}})\le a_{\max}$ with
Design on and $1/p^{\mathrm{obs}}\le a_{\max}$ with Design off, and
$1/q^{\mathrm{use}}\le d_{\max}$; when $d_{\max}\le1/q_{\min}$, as for the
frozen constants, the last clause also gives $q^{\mathrm{use}}\ge q_{\min}$.
The ceiling clause is stated on the composite weight rather than on
$1/p^{\mathrm{obs}}$: \eqref{eq:a_stabilized} already carries
$\widehat\zeta^{o}$, so bounding $1/p^{\mathrm{obs}}$ alone leaves it
unbounded.
A6a is exactly what makes the map return the ideal untruncated ratios, and
hence what makes \eqref{eq:eps_stab} vanish.

\noindent\textbf{A6b (Bounded interpolation error).}
On A5's active support the pre-outcome estimates satisfy
$|\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}-\pi^{\mathrm{opp}}|\le\varepsilon_\pi$,
$|\widehat p^{\mathrm{obs}}-p^{\mathrm{obs}}|\le\varepsilon_p$, and
$|\widehat q^{\mathrm{use}}-q^{\mathrm{use}}|\le\varepsilon_q$.

\subsection{Target Risk Is Not Arrival Risk}
\label{subsec:prop_risk}

\begin{proposition}[Target-vs-arrival risk distinction]
\label{prop:risk}
Let $\boldsymbol\varpi^{\mathrm{tar}}$ and
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathrm{arr}}$ be two normalized laws on the same
finite supported atomic domain.  If
$\boldsymbol\varpi^{\mathrm{tar}}\neq\widetilde{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathrm{arr}}$,
then the two risk functionals are not interchangeable in general: there exists
a bounded nonnegative error profile $e_i$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i
 \varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}e_i
 \neq
 \sum_i
 \widetilde\rho_i^{\mathrm{arr}}e_i.
 \label{eq:prop1_nonidentity}
\end{equation}
Conversely, equality of the two weighted expectations for every bounded error
profile implies equality of the two underlying laws.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See the supplemental material.\hfill$\square$

Proposition~\ref{prop:risk} is a functional distinction, not a claim that
every predictor or minimizer must differ numerically.

\subsection{Normalized Sequential Target Reconstruction}
\label{subsec:prop_reconstruction}

For the ideal analysis, let $\mathcal{O}_r$ denote the multiset of realized
opportunity events in window $r$.  Each event $e\in\mathcal{O}_r$ carries a
mark $(k_e,i_e)$ with marginal opportunity law
$P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k_e,i_e)$ in \eqref{eq:opp_event_law}.  Observation and
usable-update selection then occur sequentially according to
\eqref{eq:pobs_def}--\eqref{eq:quse_def}.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \chi_e
 =
 O_e U_{k_e,r}
 \frac{
 \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k_e,i_e}
 }{
 P_r^{\mathrm{opp}}(k_e,i_e)
 p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{k_e,r,i_e}
 q^{\mathrm{use}}_{k_e,r}
 }.
 \label{eq:chi_event}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Normalized target reconstruction]
\label{prop:reconstruction}
Under A1--A3, for any bounded test function $\psi(k,i)$ distinct from the
Design factor of \eqref{eq:design_factor},
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Phi_R
 =
 \frac{
 \sum_{r<R}
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal{O}_r}
 \chi_e\psi(k_e,i_e)
 }{
 \sum_{r<R}
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal{O}_r}
 \chi_e
 }
 \xrightarrow{p}
 \Phi^{\mathrm{tar}},
 \label{eq:hajek_phi}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \Phi^{\mathrm{tar}}
 =
 \sum_{k,i}
 \pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,i}\psi(k,i).
 \label{eq:target_phi}
\end{equation}
Therefore, client--stratum indicators recover
$\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{k,s}$.  By the compatibility identity
\eqref{eq:target_compatibility}, choosing
$\psi(k,i)=\mathbf{1}\{h(i)=g\}$ then recovers the deployment-group mass
$\mu_g$.  The result is moment and group-mass recovery under A1--A3, not
optimization of $\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(\theta)$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See the supplemental material.\hfill$\square$

Proposition~\ref{prop:reconstruction} identifies the ideal pooled correction
with the target-mass opportunity ratio (RAVEN-Design),
not every finite-window $\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star$; that gap is why
Section~\ref{sec:calibration} calibrates inside the current support.
It does not prove that the sequential estimator optimizes deployment-target
risk.

\subsection{Practical Plug-in Perturbation}
\label{subsec:prop_plugin}

Let $\mathcal{T}$ be the implemented stabilization map: the composition of
\eqref{eq:design_ratio_simple}, \eqref{eq:a_stabilized}, and \eqref{eq:d_use}
with the normalization \eqref{eq:beta_ref}.  Write
$\boldsymbol\beta_r^{\mathcal T}$ for the reference $\mathcal{T}$ returns at
the \emph{true} margins on $\mathcal{A}_r$, and $\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star$
for the ideal untruncated reference of the supplemental material.  The
stabilization error is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stab},r}
=
\bigl\|\boldsymbol\beta_r^{\mathcal T}-\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star\bigr\|_1 .
\label{eq:eps_stab}
\end{equation}
It vanishes exactly when $\mathcal{T}$ reproduces the ideal ratios on the
active support, and it depends only on the true margins, $\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}$
and the frozen constants, so it is measurable on a released trace.  Let
$n_r=\max_{k\in\mathcal{A}_r}\sum_i R_{k,r,i}O_{k,r,i}$ and
$K_r=|\mathcal{A}_r|$.

\begin{proposition}[Practical plug-in perturbation]
\label{prop:practical_plugin}
Assume A1, A4, A5, and A6b.  Let the stabilized window mass obey
$\mathbf{1}^{\top}b_r^{\mathcal T}\ge B_{\min}>0$ at the true margins, and let
the ideal and the implemented reference share $\mathcal{A}_r$, $M_r$, and
$\mathcal{C}_r$.  Let $\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star$ be the
same-window ideal untruncated reference and
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r$ the implemented clipped plug-in.  Let
$\boldsymbol\alpha^\star(\boldsymbol\beta)$ denote the unique P2 minimizer
at that fixed window state.  Then
\begin{align}
\bigl\|\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r-\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star\bigr\|_1
&\le
C_1\bigl(\varepsilon_\pi+\varepsilon_p+\varepsilon_q\bigr)
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stab},r},
\label{eq:g4_beta}\\
\bigl\|\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha}_r-\boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star\bigr\|_2
&\le
\bigl\|\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r-\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star\bigr\|_2,
\label{eq:g4_alpha}\\
\bigl\|M_r(\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha}_r-\boldsymbol\alpha_r^\star)\bigr\|_2
&\le
\|M_r\|_2\,
\bigl\|\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_r-\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star\bigr\|_2,
\label{eq:g4_comp}
\end{align}
where $C_1$ is the explicit Lipschitz envelope of the stabilized H\'ajek map
(supplemental material), with
$L_\pi=\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{\max}/(\pi_{\min}^2p_{\min})$,
$L_p=\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}_{\max}/(\pi_{\min}p_{\min}^2)$,
$L_q=\min\{q_{\min}^{-2},d_{\max}^2\}$, and
$C_1=(2K_rn_r/B_{\min})(d_{\max}\max\{L_\pi,L_p\}+a_{\max}L_q)$; under Design
off $\zeta\equiv1$, so $L_\pi=0$ and $L_p=1/p_{\min}^2$.  The result is a
stability bound: it does not assert unbiasedness, identification on
$\{\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}=0\}$, or robustness to a change of $\mathcal{A}_r$.  It
holds with or without A6a, which is exactly what drives
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stab},r}$ to zero.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See the supplemental material.\hfill$\square$

Two corollaries fix how the bound is read.  (a) If A6a holds,
$\boldsymbol\beta_r^{\mathcal T}=\boldsymbol\beta_r^\star$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stab},r}=0$, so \eqref{eq:g4_beta} returns to the sharp
form $C_1(\varepsilon_\pi+\varepsilon_p+\varepsilon_q)$.  (b) Under Design off
the observation side contributes nothing to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stab},r}$
exactly when $p^{\mathrm{obs}}\ge p_{\min}$ and
$1/p^{\mathrm{obs}}\le a_{\max}$, leaving the usable-update stage as the only
source.  The proposition is therefore not conditional on A6a, and since A6a
fails on the blocks of the main comparison this bound does not explain the
U-Air full-Design reversal.  $C_1$ is a worst-case envelope and not a numerical
certificate: at $\pi_{\min}=10^{-6}$ and $p_{\min}=0.05$ the Design-on
$L_\pi\le2\times10^{13}$, so the informative part of \eqref{eq:g4_beta} is
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stab},r}$.

\begin{proposition}[Group decomposition of the deployment-target criterion]
\label{prop:group_decomposition}
Partition the supported test units into groups $\mathcal G$, which may be the
groups of $h$ or any coarsening of them, and let $\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}_g$ and
$n_g$ be the evaluation mass and the unit count of group $g$.  Write
$\mathrm{RMSE}_g=[n_g^{-1}\sum_{i:h(i)=g}(\widehat Y_i-Y_i)^2]^{1/2}$ for the
equal-weight score of group $g$.  Then
$\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}^2=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}_g\,\mathrm{RMSE}_g^2$,
and along a feasible direction $d$ of the simplex, $\sum_g d_g=0$, the
directional derivative is
$\sum_g d_g\,\mathrm{RMSE}_g^2/(2\,\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu})$.  Consequently two
methods exchange order between two evaluation weight vectors
$\boldsymbol\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}$ and $\boldsymbol\mu'^{\mathrm{eval}}$ only if
$\sum_g(\mu'^{\mathrm{eval}}_g-\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}_g)
(\mathrm{RMSE}_{g,a}^2-\mathrm{RMSE}_{g,b}^2)$ changes sign.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See the supplemental material.\hfill$\square$

\noindent The decomposition rests on three stated conditions: the groups
partition the supported test units, the within-group weights do not depend on
the other groups' masses, and $\boldsymbol\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}$ is a probability
vector.  It re-weights one fixed set of predictions, so it constrains how a
reported value moves with the target map and not how a method would perform
under a different map, and the group scores remain measured.  For the two
Design settings scored under one map it decides the prediction endpoint: the
sign of $\sum_g\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}_g[\mathrm{RMSE}_g^2(\mathrm{on})-
\mathrm{RMSE}_g^2(\mathrm{off})]$ is the sign of the
$\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ difference, and it says nothing about the structural
endpoints $\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$ and $\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$.

\subsection{Aggregate Coverage Guarantees}
\label{subsec:agg_coverage_theory}

The reconstruction results above do not by themselves yield a finite-sample
coverage certificate.  Deterministic pre-window pairwise LCBs are vacuous
under hidden zero-opportunity events, and the frozen stress levels
$a\in\{0.80,0.60,0.40,0.20\}$ fall in that region
(Section~\ref{subsec:pairwise_barrier}).  In a certified regime the
false-safe probability of an empirical diagnostic Gate is bounded by
$\alpha$, by the false-safe Gate corollary in the supplemental material; and
no method can simultaneously provide nontrivial per-window certification and
universal protection against arbitrary unannounced regime shifts, by the
arbitrary-drift applicability boundary proposition in the supplemental
material.

\begin{assumption}[Local score exchangeability within a certified stable regime]
\label{ass:score-exchangeability}
Conditioned on the frozen public regime descriptor $g$, the current score
$S_t$ and the $n$ historical calibration scores are exchangeable.  Operational
regime certification does not prove this assumption; it only defines the
pre-registered subset of windows for which the assumption is posited.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Stable-regime aggregate lower coverage]
\label{thm:agg-lcb}
Assume window $t$ is in operational state CERTIFIED, $n\ge n_{\min}=19$,
Assumption~\ref{ass:score-exchangeability} holds, $\widehat C_t=f_b(F_{t-})$
uses the frozen predictor snapshot of the current block, and the calibration
scores are strictly historical same-block scores.  Let
$q_t=S_{(k_n)}^{\mathrm{cal}}$ with $k_n$ from \eqref{eq:agg_kn}, and define
$C^{\mathrm{LCB}}_t$ by \eqref{eq:agg_CLCB}.  Then
\begin{equation}
 P\bigl(C^{\mathrm{LCB}}_t\le C_t\mid g\bigr)\ge 1-\alpha.
 \label{eq:agg_thm1}
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=0.05$ the right-hand side is $0.95$.  The theorem remains
conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:score-exchangeability} even when the
algorithmic state is CERTIFIED.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
See the supplemental material.\hfill$\square$

If Assumption~\ref{ass:score-exchangeability} holds, Theorem~\ref{thm:agg-lcb}
bounds the Gate in that certified regime.  Adaptive RESET/CERTIFIED switching
is not proven valid, and Algorithm~\ref{alg:window_close} does not open Design
from $\mathrm{Gate}_t$.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate RAVEN-MCS on risk interchangeability, RAVEN-Design versus
the RAVEN-NoDesign setting, and component roles.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Risk interchangeability:} does two-stage non-random selection
    create a deployment-target mismatch, including under real taxi opportunity?
    \item \textbf{RAVEN-Design and RAVEN-NoDesign:} does RAVEN-Design reduce
    structural mismatch on the protocol-$R$ development table, and how do the
    two settings trade off against each other and against prediction?
    \item \textbf{Component roles:} which terms move structural diagnostics
    versus prediction, and when is Design useful as a reconstruction factor?
\end{itemize}

The balanced control is a no-harm check, and a risk-interchangeability
experiment tests whether arrival-weighted and deployment-target-weighted risks
are interchangeable.  Independent rankings follow on T-Drive taxi replay,
NSW-Traffic regions, and City Scanner occupancy with controlled or measured
$O$; none of those rows is mixed into
Table~\ref{tab:main_effectiveness}.  The main comparison is a development table
under always-on protocol $R$ on station traces, not a jointly measured mobile
ranking; an intermittent-occupancy protocol table on the same station clients
is in the supplemental material.  A component-removal ablation drops one
component at a time: on SensorScope, Design moves $\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$
while $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ stays essentially flat, so
$\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$ is a structural diagnostic.

\subsubsection{Selection protocol and compared methods}

The main comparison and ablation condition is \emph{Complete-aligned}: protocol $R$, $O$,
and $U$ are all active and non-random in the same direction.  On

The main blocks assume within-stratum exchangeability,

\subsubsection{Metrics and statistical policy}

Prediction is measured on the supported test units by
\[
 \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}
 =
 \Bigl[\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{test}}}
 \frac{\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}_{h(i)}}{n_{h(i)}}
 \bigl(\widehat Y_i-Y_i\bigr)^2\Bigr]^{1/2},
\]
where $n_g$ is the test-unit count in group $g$, $\boldsymbol\mu$ is the
deployment-target marginal $\mu_g=\sum_{i:h(i)=g}\varpi_i^{\mathrm{tar}}$,
$\boldsymbol\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}$ is the evaluation group mass normalized on
the supported test units, and $\boldsymbol\mu^{\mathrm{blk}}$ aggregates
$\boldsymbol\mu$ to the four UTC blocks.  On SensorScope and U-Air
$\boldsymbol\mu^{\mathrm{eval}}$ is uniform on the supported test units, so
$\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ is the unweighted pooled RMSE.
The U-Air and NSW-Traffic targets are standardized by their training-split
scales ($\sigma=17.363$ and $575.776$), so those $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ entries
are dimensionless while SensorScope is reported in physical units; ratios of two
runs, such as $\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$ and $\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$, are invariant
to this standardization.  $\mathrm{WorstGroupRMSE}$ is the largest of the four
UTC-block scores, each restricting the same weights to its own test units and
renormalizing them inside the block (per-group table in the supplemental
material), and the under-served-block score uses the block whose
$\mu^{\mathrm{blk}}_b$ most exceeds opportunity mass
(Section~\ref{subsec:eval_underserved}).  $\mathrm{Tail\_RMSE}$ applies that
block functional to the frozen late-day blocks $2$ and $3$ ($12$--$24$~UTC),
fixed before the runs rather than ranked by error or arrival ratio.  For the
risk-interchangeability test only, $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\rho}$ replaces the evaluation
weights by the atomic arrival weights of \eqref{eq:arrival_distribution}.
Structural alignment is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}(R)=\left\|\overline{\boldsymbol\omega}_R-\boldsymbol\mu\right\|_1$
and $\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$ in \eqref{eq:delta_cs}; smaller is better.

All formal method comparisons are paired by seed, using the paired Wilcoxon
protocol~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual} with Holm
correction~\cite{holm1979simple} within the predeclared comparison families at
$\alpha=0.05$; absolute differences below $10^{-12}$ are numerical ties, and
tables report mean $\pm$ standard deviation.  On four-block
$\mathrm{WorstGroupRMSE}$, RAVEN-Design (SensorScope) and RAVEN-NoDesign (U-Air)
are each compared with FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window by a pre-specified $+0.5\%$
mean-relative point-estimate tolerance on the paired relative differences, using
TEST seeds $30011$--$30020$ for the ranking prose and the four 20-seed
comparisons for the Holm tests.  The tolerance is descriptive rather than a
formal non-inferiority test, the U-Air comparison is a fallback evaluation rather
than a target-mass claim, and the under-served-block check applies the same
$0.5\%$ rule on validation only.  For P2, warning-affected allocations satisfy
the recorded feasibility constraints; the stored outputs do not support
retrospective certification of exact numerical optimality.

\subsection{Risk Interchangeability under Two-Stage Selection}
\label{subsec:eval_rq1}

Under the balanced control, TimeAlign obtains mean deployment-target
RMSE $7.7108\pm0.0272$ and RAVEN $7.7154\pm0.0337$ (mean relative
degradation $0.0599\%$; one-sided 95\% upper bound $0.134\%$, below the
3\% no-harm margin).  The balanced control is a sanity check, not a superiority test.

We then test the premise of Proposition~1 on the NSW-Traffic
risk-interchangeability experiment.  For each method and seed, write
$\Delta_{\mu}=\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{aligned}}
-\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{balanced}}$ and
$\Delta_{\rho}=\mathrm{RMSE}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{aligned}}
-\mathrm{RMSE}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{balanced}}$, with the pre-specified
difference-of-differences $D_{\mathrm{aligned}}=\Delta_{\mu}-\Delta_{\rho}$,
which would concentrate around zero if the two risk views reacted
interchangeably to the same selection shift.  Instead,
Fig.~\ref{fig:risk_noninterchangeability} shows a stable negative shift for all
five methods: mean $D_{\mathrm{aligned}}$ ranges from $-0.0209$ to $-0.0214$,
17--18 of the 20 paired seeds are negative for each method, and the paired
Wilcoxon tests against zero give $p=4.83\times10^{-4}$--$5.86\times10^{-4}$.  The
mean target-risk change is only about $8\times10^{-4}$ against a mean arrival-risk
change of about $2.2\times10^{-2}$, so the same two-stage selection shift changes
the arrival-weighted and deployment-target-weighted views by materially different
amounts.  Complete method-level statistics are in the risk-semantic appendix of
the supplemental material.

\paragraph{Setting the Design factor.}
A deployment that needs one setting rather than the full factorial can fix it
before the window opens from public information, in two steps.  The first is a
support gate: writing $n^{\mathrm{zero}}_r$ for the delivered training risk units
of block $r$ whose attached client--stratum carries zero public target mass and
$n_r$ for all delivered units, define
\begin{equation}
\rho_r=\frac{n^{\mathrm{zero}}_r}{n_r},
\qquad
D_r=\mathrm{on}\iff\rho_r=0 .
\label{eq:design_gate}
\end{equation}
Design is on exactly when no delivered unit sits on a stratum that the public
target map declares empty.  On the reported blocks the gate returns on for
SensorScope, NSW-Traffic and both City Scanner rankings, and off for U-Air
($35.4\%$) and T-Drive ($47.4\%$), the settings those rankings use.  The gate is
a stopping condition rather than a preference between endpoints, withholding the
factor where the ablation measures its largest effect, the $6.943\%$ of
$\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$ and $6.885\%$ of $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ that Design costs
on U-Air (Section~\ref{subsec:eval_rq2}).  The second step is the switch point.
Writing $M_{e,r}(D)$ for endpoint
$e\in\mathcal{E}=\{\Delta_{\mathrm{group}},\Delta_{c\text{-}s},
\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu},\mathrm{Tail}\}$ under setting $D$, and weighting the four
endpoints relative to their Design-on values,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat J_r(D)&=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}w_e\,\frac{M_{e,r}(D)}{M_{e,r}(\mathrm{on})},\\
D_r^{\star}&=\arg\min_{D\in\{\mathrm{on},\mathrm{off}\}}\widehat J_r(D),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:design_switch}
\end{equation}
the two settings are indifferent when
$w_c\,4.990=w_g\,6.943+w_\mu\,6.885+w_t\,7.221$, so a deployment that reads
structure only keeps Design on if and only if $w_c/w_g>1.39$, one that reads
$\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ alone keeps it off, and on SensorScope all four terms move
the same way, so Design on is optimal for every weight vector.  The switch point
is a decision standard for blocks of this kind rather than a universal law, and
Table~\ref{tab:e4_ablation} records what the gate forgoes on U-Air, the $4.990\%$
$\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$ gain.  Proposition~\ref{prop:group_decomposition} supplies
the block decomposition behind the $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ entries of this
trade-off, so a deployment can read the prediction side of the switch from the
per-group scores it already reports, though that decomposition covers neither
$\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$ nor $\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$.

\paragraph{Observation and usable-update correction.}
Observation correction has a smaller, endpoint-specific effect: removing Obs
worsens $\Delta_{\mathrm{group}}$ by $0.123\%$ on U-Air (Holm-significant) while
its $\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$ and prediction effects are not significant, and on
SensorScope no group change is significant while $\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$ improves
by $0.070\%$.  Removing Use produces no significant change in any core endpoint
on either dataset, so the ablation does not establish its marginal
indispensability in this Complete-aligned family: the selection layer remains
distinct in the factorization, but its isolated empirical effect is not
established here.

\paragraph{Instantaneous calibration and debt.}
Inst also trades endpoints: on SensorScope removing it worsens
$\Delta_{c\text{-}s}$ by $0.310\%$ (significant) while the group change is not
significant after Holm correction, and on U-Air the structural changes are not
significant while removing Inst worsens $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ and tail RMSE by
$0.143\%$ and $0.149\%$ (both $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.0194$).  Debt has the
narrowest but most consistent role: removing it significantly increases both
structural mismatches on both datasets, but only by $0.0141\%/0.0040\%$ on
SensorScope and $0.0043\%/0.0024\%$ on U-Air, well below the $0.5\%$
paired-relative minimum practical effect used elsewhere.  This matches the
accounting of \eqref{eq:debt_bound} rather than a standalone performance driver,
and does not establish $\|\mathbf Q_R\|_1=o(S_R)$.

\noindent\textbf{Server-weight rules trained under the same policy.}
Retraining the same window policy while changing only the server weights
separates structural movement from prediction.  With 20 paired seeds per dataset
the group endpoint orders the same way on both datasets, with the
projection-with-debt rule lowest, then the projection alone, frozen RAVEN and
instantaneous calibration, uniform weights, and count-weighted FedAvg: $0.98464$,
$1.01158$, $1.01394$, $1.01409$, $1.02693$, and $1.05577$ on SensorScope, and
$1.08001$, $1.09162$, $1.09236$, $1.09241$, $1.09734$, and $1.12176$ on U-Air.
Paired within each dataset, the projection improves on uniform weights by
$+0.768$ pp ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=2.7\times10^{-5}$) on SensorScope, the value the
read-only ladder above also gives, and by $+0.286$ pp ($0.0166$) on U-Air; the
debt recursion adds $+1.347$ pp and $+0.580$ pp (Holm $\approx10^{-5}$ on both);
and frozen RAVEN trails the projection by $0.118$ pp on SensorScope ($0.019$) and
is indistinguishable from it on U-Air.  These projection rules are new rules of
this study, not the frozen RAVEN configuration, and they cost no wall-clock time:
$18.5$--$20.0$~s against $27.1$~s on SensorScope.  No prediction-endpoint
contrast survives Holm correction on either dataset (SensorScope
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1.000$ throughout; U-Air best raw $p=0.027$,
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.133$), and the arm order on $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ does not
follow the structural order, which is what the ceiling measurement of
Section~\ref{subsec:ceiling} anticipates.

\subsection{Recoverable-Gain Ceiling}
\label{subsec:ceiling}

The recoverable gain is bounded inside the class the method defines: weights in
$\mathcal{C}_r$ of \eqref{eq:Cr}, selected by (P2), scored by the
deployment-target criterion $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$.  Two measured quantities bound
it.  The first is the headroom from the uncorrected member (FedAvg-Window) to the
best member evaluated on true margins,
$H=(\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}(\text{FedAvg})-\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}(\text{oracle}))/\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}(\text{FedAvg})$,
over a stress grid varying the opportunity tilt, the tail observation scale, the
model capacity, and the target.  The GO rule, frozen before the runs, required
some condition to reach $H\ge0.5\%$ with a positive interval lower bound, and it
did not fire.  SensorScope is conclusive at a paired-difference minimum
detectable effect of $0.17$--$0.36$ percentage points, with all six estimates
inside $[-0.03\%,+0.08\%]$.  U-Air was re-run at $20$ seeds, $270$ runs and $45$
regenerated traces, where every estimate satisfies $|H|\le0.217\%$ with a $95\%$
interval containing zero; its realized detectable effect of $0.70$--$0.80$ points
excludes effects above about $0.7\%$ without resolving the $0.5\%$ bar on its
own.  Hardening the observation propensity to vary within a stratum and correlate
with the label raises the oracle gap by $15$--$21\times$, to $0.104\%$ on
SensorScope and $0.251\%$ on U-Air, both inside the same bound, so the gap is a
property of these endpoints rather than of an over-easy regime.

The second quantity is the cost of estimating the margins, measured inside one
calibrated member so that only the observation and usable-update margins change.
Replacing those two margins with their true values moves $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mu}$ by
$-0.008\%$ on SensorScope (oracle minus calibrated,
$95\%$ interval $[-0.041\%,+0.025\%]$) and by $+0.040\%$ on U-Air
($[-0.090\%,+0.171\%]$), paired over $20$ seeds.  Every member we evaluate stays
inside $0.08\%$ on SensorScope under the same substitution, and on U-Air three of
the four stay inside $0.3\%$ while TwoStage-H\'ajek sits $2.04\%$ above the
oracle-margin member ($[1.33\%,2.75\%]$).  The substitution is not small before
aggregation: the estimated reference differs from the true-margin reference by a
median of $0.077$ in $\ell_1$ and by up to $1.15$, and it reaches the aggregate
composition at the same order for the members whose weights normalize that
reference.  Accurate margin estimates are therefore not what holds the prediction
endpoint flat, since three of the four members are insensitive to a large change
in their own aggregation weights.  At the composition level the perturbation is
already bounded, because \eqref{eq:g4_comp} limits the aggregate difference
induced by the margin estimates to $\|M_r\|_2$ times the reference perturbation
controlled by Proposition~\ref{prop:practical_plugin}; a metric-level bound of
the same form would require a transfer assumption between the training and the
deployment distributions, which Proposition~2 does not make, so the metric-level
ceiling above is measured, at the minimum detectable effects stated.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

RAVEN-Design and the City Scanner blocks report Design on, and RAVEN-NoDesign
reports Design off on U-Air and T-Drive ($\widehat\zeta^{o}=1$); both settings are
reported where the trade-off is material.  On
Table~\ref{tab:main_effectiveness}, SensorScope RAVEN-Design and U-Air
RAVEN-NoDesign stay within the descriptive $+0.5\%$ TEST tolerance versus
FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window, and we claim no Holm-significant RMSE win.  That
table is a development table under always-on protocol $R$ on station traces, not
a real-mobility ranking; Table~\ref{tab:tdrive_hybrid} is outside the tolerance,
and FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window remain strongest under real taxi $R$.  The
NSW-Traffic four-region, City Scanner visit-occupancy, and Pilot~2 measured-$O$
rankings of the supplemental material are not mixed into
Table~\ref{tab:main_effectiveness}.  The Design factor is an explicit
configuration on each block's own $\pi^{\mathrm{tar}}$ map, neither setting is
derived from a data statistic, and neither verifies positivity on
target-positive pairs.  A1--A2 remain support and confounding boundaries, and
A6a does not hold on the released traces, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:practical_plugin} is used in its
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stab}}$ form \eqref{eq:eps_stab}.  The $25.5\%$ figure is
EventTrace occurrence ($13040$ of $51200$), not the target-coverage statistic of
the supplemental material.  On a frozen coverage-stress oracle, pooled CERTIFIED
validity is $0.959$ and U-Air $a=1.00$ is $0.750$; adaptive RESET/CERTIFIED
switching is not proven valid, and Design is not opened from
$\mathrm{Gate}_t$.  Federated learning itself does not provide a privacy
guarantee.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Windowed-asynchronous federated MCS completion should align selected mass
with the public deployment target, not with the arrival law of usable
updates.  RAVEN-Design reconstructs compatible target mass and calibrates that
reference inside the current feasible window, while RAVEN-NoDesign uses
$\widehat\zeta=1$ and does not reconstruct target mass on that layer; when the
two laws differ, their risks are not interchangeable.  The ideal sequential
correction recovers compatible target moments or group mass under A1--A3 without
proving optimization of deployment-target risk, calibration is uniquely solvable,
and the clipped plug-in is a stable perturbation of the same-window reference
when target support is compatible.

On the protocol development table, most of the FedAvg structural gap on both
datasets is recoverable by uniform server weights, and the surviving part
requires the reconstructed reference projected onto the feasible set.
SensorScope RAVEN-Design reports structural alignment and a descriptive $+0.5\%$
worst-group tolerance, not an RMSE win; U-Air and T-Drive report RAVEN-NoDesign.
Hybrid tables are mobility ceilings, not confirmations of
Table~\ref{tab:main_effectiveness}: FedAU-Window and ObsUse-Window remain
strongest on T-Drive, FedAvg-Window is lowest on the City Scanner visit-occupancy
ranking, and TwoStage-H\'ajek is lowest on Pilot~2 measured $O$.  $O$ and $U$ are
protocol-constructed except Pilot~2 measured $O$; usable-update correction has no
isolated effect in the tested family; target debt remains internal accounting; and
no ranking jointly measures $R$, $O$, and $U$.
